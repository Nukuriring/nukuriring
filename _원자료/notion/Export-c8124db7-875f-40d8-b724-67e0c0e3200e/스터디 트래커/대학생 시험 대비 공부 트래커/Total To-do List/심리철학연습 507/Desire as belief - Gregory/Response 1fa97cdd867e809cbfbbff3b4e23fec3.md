# Response

# **(1) Motivation Argument, Non-Cognitivism, and the Burden on Conativism**

## 1. **The Motivation Argument and Its Initial Dialectical Tension**

Gregory begins with an argument designed to reconcile two widely held views about human motivation:

- **Motivation Internalism**: *Normative attitudes can motivate us to act.*
- **ODM (Only Desires Motivate)**: *Only desires, not beliefs alone, can be the source of motivation.*

These jointly imply:

> Conclusion: Normative attitudes are desires.
> 

In logical form:

> (P1) Normative attitudes motivate.
> 
> 
> (P2) Only desires motivate.
> 
> ∴ Normative attitudes are desires.
> 

Gregory names this the **Motivation Argument**, and it is this inference that provides the entry point for a long-standing metaethical debate: how best to explain the motivational efficacy of normative judgments.

---

## 2. **Non-Cognitivism and Its Seeming Fit with the Motivation Argument**

Historically, many **non-cognitivists** (e.g. Blackburn, Gibbard, Hare) have interpreted this argument as direct support for their view. For the non-cognitivist, normative judgments are not genuine beliefs aimed at truth but rather **expressions of desire-like mental states**—attitudes not governed by truth-apt semantics but by affective or conative force.

From this perspective, the conclusion of the Motivation Argument—that normative attitudes are desires—fits hand-in-glove with **non-cognitivist metaphysics**.

> Thus, the Motivation Argument seems, at first glance, to license non-cognitivism.
> 

But Gregory emphasizes a **key internal constraint**: for non-cognitivism to truly draw on the Motivation Argument, it must endorse a strong **identity thesis**—namely:

> Conativism: All normative attitudes are desires.
> 

This is **not optional**. If the non-cognitivist accepts both premises of the Motivation Argument (especially the claim that only desires motivate), then to explain the motivational power of normative judgments, they **must** reduce them all to desires.

Put simply:

- **If** normative judgments can motivate (P1),
- **and if** only desires can motivate (P2),
- **then** the non-cognitivist must say: *All normative judgments are desires*.

Otherwise, they would have to deny either ODM or Internalism—both of which Gregory takes to be independently plausible.

---

## 3. **The Pressure on Conativism: What the Motivation Argument Actually Shows**

Here, Gregory introduces a decisive **dialectical shift**. While the Motivation Argument does entail that **some** normative attitudes are desires (those that motivate), it **does not entail** that *all* normative attitudes are desires. And this distinction is pivotal.

For Gregory, the class of normative judgments that motivate us is **restricted** to **first-personal normative attitudes**—judgments about what *I* ought to do, or what *I* have reason to do. These are the kinds of judgments that guide action in deliberation. In contrast, **other-regarding normative attitudes**—judgments about what *others* ought to do—often fail to motivate the subject who holds them.

Thus:

> The Motivation Argument supports at most a restricted identity claim:
> 
> 
> *Only* first-personal normative attitudes are desires.
> 
> Conativism, which universalizes this claim, **overreaches**.
> 

---

## 4. **Why Conativism Must Be Rejected—and What Follows**

If Conativism is false, then non-cognitivism collapses. For Gregory, **Conativism is not merely a side-feature** of non-cognitivism—it is its **motivational core**. Once one concedes that some normative attitudes (e.g. “Mo ought to sing Happy Birthday”) are **not** desires—because they neither motivate nor are they constituted by any motivational state—then the core metaphysical commitment of non-cognitivism (that normative attitudes are conative rather than cognitive) is **no longer defensible**.

Therefore:

> Conativism fails.Motivation Argument does not entail non-cognitivism.Non-cognitivism loses its best (and perhaps only) argument.
> 

---

## 5. **Why Desire-as-Belief Emerges as the Better Alternative**

At this point, Gregory offers **Desire-as-Belief (DaB)** as the more precise and elegant conclusion to draw from the Motivation Argument. DaB states:

> Desire-as-Belief: To desire that p is to believe that one has reason to bring about p.
> 

This view satisfies both premises of the Motivation Argument:

- It retains ODM: only desires motivate.
- It affirms motivation internalism: reasons-beliefs (a subset of normative beliefs) **do** motivate.
- But crucially, it restricts the identity thesis: **only** first-personal reasons-beliefs are desires. Others are just beliefs.

Thus, Desire-as-Belief **preserves the structure** of the Motivation Argument without committing to the universal reduction required by Conativism.

In short:

> Rejecting Conativism is not the end of the Motivation Argument.
> 
> 
> **It is the beginning of the proper conclusion: Desire-as-Belief.**
>

# ❖ Gregory’s Rejection of Conativism: A Structured Dialectical Reconstruction

---

## ① 정의: Conativism의 입장과 부담

**Conativism**은 다음과 같은 보편적 동일시 주장을 포함한다:

> Conativism: All normative attitudes are desires.
> 

이 주장은 특히 **non-cognitivists**에게 필연적이다.

Motivation Argument의 두 전제:

- **Motivation Internalism**: Normative attitudes can motivate.
- **ODM**: Only desires can motivate.

이 둘을 모두 받아들이는 비인지주의자는 필연적으로:

> ∴ All normative attitudes are desires (Conativism)
> 

을 받아들여야 한다 (§3.3.1 중반–후반).

하지만 Gregory는 이 일반화가 과도하다고 주장하며, 아래와 같은 반례 논변을 전개한다.

---

## ② 첫 반론: 타인지향적 규범 판단은 욕구가 아니다

**핵심 주장:**

> Other-regarding normative attitudes lack motivational force.
> 
- 예시: Tanya가 “Mo는 아들에게 노래해야 한다”고 믿더라도,
    
    그녀는 **그 일이 일어나기를 바라지 않을 수 있다.**
    
- 더 나아가, Ursula, Sara, 그리고 Prisoner’s Dilemma 사례는 모두:
    - 타인의 규범적 행위에 대한 판단
    - 하지만 **아무런 행동 유도 욕구를 가지지 않음**

**귀결:**

> Motivation Argument는 최대한 일인칭 규범 태도에만 적용 가능.
> 
> 
> 따라서 Motivation Argument는 Conativism을 정당화하지 않음. (§3.3.2 전체)
> 

---

## ③ 첫 재반론: 비난 욕구로의 환원

**Conativist의 시도:**

> 타인지향적 규범 판단은
> 
> 
> ⟶ “그가 그 행위를 하지 않을 경우, 나는 **비난하고 싶다**”는 욕구로 환원 가능
> 

즉, **동기 유발 기능은 ‘행위 자체’가 아닌 ‘비난하려는 행위’로 나타난다.** (§3.3.3 초반)

---

## ④ 재재반론: 비난 욕구 동일시의 실패

**Gregory의 반박 요지:**

1. **비난 없는 규범 판단이 가능**
    - “그는 잘못했다. 하지만 비난할 이유는 없다.” (예: Blameless wrongdoing)
2. **비도덕적 규범 판단은 비난 욕구와 무관**
    - 예: 신중함(“그는 운동화를 사야 한다”), 미학적 판단(“그 그림을 걸어야 한다”)
3. **외부 사례 가능성**
    - 외계 문명: 비난이라는 개념 자체 없음
    - 도구적 공리주의자: **비난은 비생산적**이므로 욕구하지 않음

**결론:**

> 비난 욕구는 규범 판단의 보편적 형식이 될 수 없다.
> 
> 
> ∴ 타인지향적 규범 판단은 여전히 욕구로 환원되지 않는다 (§3.3.3 후반)
> 

---

## ⑤ 두 번째 재반론: Gibbard의 조건적 욕구 이론

**Gibbard’s Proposal:**

> “A thinks B ought to φ”
> 
> 
> ⟶ “A has a **conditional desire**: if A were B, in B’s situation, A would φ.”
> 

이러한 조건적 욕구는 타인지향적 판단을 **간접적으로 욕구로 환원**하려는 시도이다.

Gibbard는 이를 통해 Motivation Argument의 귀결을 구제하고자 한다. (§3.3.4 도입부)

---

## ⑥ 두 번째 재재반론 1: Gibbard-simple에 대한 반박

Gregory의 첫 반박 대상은:

> Gibbard-simple:
> 
> 
> A의 판단 [B ought to φ] = A의 욕구 [If A were in B’s circumstances, A would φ]
> 

**문제점:**

1. **심리적으로 부자연스러움**
    - 규범 판단이 “내가 그의 상황이라면”을 조건으로 할 이유 없음
2. **인지적으로 판단 간 구별 붕괴**
    - [B ought to φ] ≠ [I ought to φ if I were in B’s circumstances]
    - 우리는 이 둘 사이에서 **추론**, **설득** 등을 수행함
3. **Supervenience는 심리 상태를 설명하지 못함**
    - Supervenience는 **세계의 구조에 관한 명제**일 뿐,
        - 개인이 반드시 그 귀결을 믿고 있어야 한다는 심리적 전제는 아님 (§3.3.4 중반)

---

## ⑦ 두 번째 재재반론 2: Gibbard-sophisticated에 대한 반박

**Gibbard-sophisticated**:

> “A believes B ought to φ”
> 
> 
> ⟶ “A desires: If I were B, and in B’s circumstances, I would φ”
> 

이는 haecceity(정체성)를 바꾸는 조건적 욕구.

**문제점:**

1. **여전히 판단 구별 무너뜨림**
    - [B ought to φ] ≠ [I ought to φ if I were B]
2. **형이상학적 부담이 더 크다**
    - “내가 그 사람이라면”이라는 **존재적 전환 조건**은 매우 이질적인 심리 상태
3. **발달심리학적 비자연성**
    - 어린이는 이런 형이상학적 동일시 없이도 타인을 판단함

**결론:**

> Gibbard-sophisticated 역시 설명력, 심리적 자연성, 인지 구조 면에서 모두 실패 (§3.3.5 전체)
>
