Inference: Neither sufficient nor necessary for concept possession

In this paper, Williamson tries to argue that inferentialist account of concept possession fails. For this purpose, he gives out several examples that undermine attempts to make accepting the inference a necessary condition for having the concept. He first considers Logical unorthodoxy cases where one is still capable of possessing a logical concept even though he rejects the relevant inferring patterns for various reasons. He then turns to the pejorative terms, which is used as a counterexample of (MEC) by Boghssian. He denies that pejorative terms indeed yield counterexample of (MEC) for the fact that (MEC) is vacuously true. After that, he moves on to consider the other type of possible counterexamples – terms that artificially stipulated as relevant inference rules are constitutive of possession condition of the concept. - introduced by Boghossian. Again, he argues that these terms fail to yield genuine counterexamples of (MEC), thus do not give a motive for Boghossian to modify (MEC). On the section 5,6 and 7, Williamson examines Boghossian’s account of blameless inference which relies on the relations between entitlements, defectiveness and conditionalization.

On the first section, Williamson rejects the connection which holds between concept possession and willing to infer according to relevant inference pattern by considering McGee and McGee’s student cases. He first clears that understanding word’s meaning is sufficient condition of having the concept. McGee, who is an expert logician, rejects some instances of Modus Ponens for theoretical reason. Still, he is perfectly able to understand the meaning of ‘if’. Doesn’t that mean he still has a concept if, for understanding the word’s meaning is a sufficient condition for having the concept? Consider P as a certain inference pattern, and C for the relevant concept and Inst as a particular instance of P.

An Expert on C and P understands the word ‘C’ which means C

This expert can reject Inst for some complicated theoretical reason. (or by a mistake)

However, He still understands ‘C’, for that he has the concept C

Therefore, willing to infer according to Inst is not the possession condition for C

Any instances of P can be counted as Inst

What if we say this expert has a disposition to infer according to Inst, but that disposition has been overridden by another disposition? And take this defeasible disposition as very possession condition for concept we were looking for. It will not help because the putative disposition also fails to construct possession condition for a concept, because one still seems to be able to have the concept without the envisaged disposition.

Williamson now turns to the pejoratives. Boghossian treats ‘boche’ as counterexample to (MEC). according to (MEC), one is immediately entitled to concept constitutive inferences. However, in the case of ‘boche’, it seems that one is not so entitled to the relevant inference which can be expressed as “x is German / x is cruel”. This objection only works if one should infer according to the relevant inference rules in order to have the concept boche.

Suppose Inferring according to relevant rules is a indeed precondition for having the concept boche. one will be committed to the epistemically suspicious claim “Germans are cruel”, for just having the concept boche. Williamson employs Gricean notion of Conventional Implicature to give account of how one can have the given concept while not being involved in epsitemically suspicious claim. One is engaged with the Conventional Implicature of the concept only in the case one actually uses the word ‘boche’. Thus, one needs not to be entitled to epistemically suspicious claim for just having the concept boche.

After all, It is implausible to think that willing to infer according to certain inferential rules is precondition for having the relevant concept. We are still able to have a concept while not willing to infer according to the relevant rules. This was shown above.

Because there is no such inferential transitions which are built into the possession condition of a concept, (MEC) is vacuously true. Therefore, It cannot help to explain our inferential entitlements.

In the section 4, Williamson argues even stipulating artificial possession conditions will not help Boghossian to avoid previous arguments. Trying to give out necessary and sufficient possession condition for artificially stipulated concept will not succeed because (1) artificially stipulated concepts do not seem to be genuine concept which we can really have. (2) even if we suppose that they are genuine concept, problem from section 2 occurs again.

Now he considers Boghossian’s Neutrino case. Boghossian claims that willingness to affirm the Carnap sentence for having the concept neutrino. Carnap sentence is something like this.

If one has to assents to the (M) in order to possess the concept, the concept is conditionalized. Boghossian’s claim can be easily rejected by constructing similar argument from section 2 of this paper.

Expert physicist might be mistaken and reject (M).

Although his reasoning for rejecting (M) may be unsound, it does not seem to show that he lacks the concept neutrino.

Inferentialist keeps on finding themselves in this impoverishment because they expect to find an inference necessary and sufficient condition for knowing what a word means. Williamson claims that there is no such litmus paper for knowing what a word means or having a concept.

On section 6, he introduces the notions of Unique characterizations and Unique Realizations. Good rules for a new concept should characterize the new concept uniquely. In other words, any two expressions subject to the rules are provably equivalent to each other. Possibly, one might also hope new concept to be uniquely realized. In other words, the concept would uniquely determine its reference across all logically possible cases.

Conditionalized concept does not guarantee relevant theory to be uniquely characterized. A theory needs some additional sentences in itself to be uniquely characterized. If we add the required sentences to the theory, it is provable that unique characterization condition holds for the conditionalized concept if and only if unique characterization condition holds for the unconditionalized concept. Thus, if unconditionalized concept fails to satisfy unique characterization, conditionalized concept also fails. If we want a non-defective concept which satisfies unique characterization, we must in general modify the possession condition again. (unique realization condition does not hold for the conditionalized concept too, hence, need for modification.)

With appropriate modification, we can gain a possession condition which satisfies unique characterization and unique realization. However, the problems are that Boghossian employs introduction and elimination rules for the neutrino concept. Unfortunately, Boghossian’s introduction and elimination rules and appropriately modified possession condition are not compatible on treating multiple realizations.

Above consideration shows that it is quite a complex matter to construct a theory which uniquely characterizes and realizes from conditionalized, non-defective concepts. This complexity reinforces earlier doubts. (1) It is not obvious that expert physicists will know how to construct a theory from the given concepts. However, without their positive affirmations, Boghossian can’t explain how they possess such concepts. (2) For someone who is an expert on both concept possession and physics, there is still a chance for him to be mistaken. Thus, he mistakenly rejects an inference rule assent to which is precondition for understanding the concept. Nevertheless, he can perfectly understand the word, and communicate with other scientists.

Finally, Williamson disagrees with Boghossian on the non-defective unconditionalized concept. He believes Boghossian was too quick to make a conclusion. Since it is possible to formulate the concept of basic logical operators without using logical operators, Boghossian was too quick to say that unconditionalized concept of basic logical operators are non-defective. Of course there are some upshots from employing conditionalized versions of logical concepts. It will yield intuitionalist logic. However, the fact we get weaker system if we use conditionalized version of logical concept does not mean it is impossible. Thus, Boghossian’s claim that there is no option left for the logical concept except employing unconditionalized version of the concept is false.

윌리암슨은 ‘boche’에 대해서 다루는 파트에서 다음과 같은 언급을 남긴다.

“Fully to understand a word, one must have some awareness, however inexplicit, of the conventional implicatures that it generates.”

한 단어를 온전히 이해하는 것에는 그 단어가 생산해내는 관습적 함축에 대한 주체의 awareness가 포함되어 있어야만 한다는 주장처럼 보인다. 그렇다면, 도대체 관습적 함축(혹은 대화적 함축)이 단어, 혹은 발화와 맺고 있는 관계란 무엇인가? 윌리암슨은 여기서 의미에 대한 넓은 의미에서 관습적 함축들도 의미의 일부라고 인정하고 있다. (The conventional implicatures that words such as ‘but’ and ‘Boche’ generate are part of their meaning, in a broad sense of ‘meaning’.)

그라이스의 함축이론에 대한 설명에서도 볼 수 있듯이, 함축은 detachable 하고, cancellable하다. 윌리암슨은 개별화된 개념 입장에 대해 다음과 같은 비판을 전개한다.

“On this view, when our unorthodox thinker refuses to infer any longer according to P, she associates the word ‘C’ with a new concept, because she changes the associated inferential role, while retaining the same meaning for the same word. Hence we cannot properly give the meaning of ‘C’ by saying that it is used to express the concept C, for the association with C is not essential to that meaning. Thus the word ‘C’ can hardly be said to express the concept C even in the mouth of a speaker who does in fact associate ‘C’ with C.”

이 비판의 핵심은 ‘C’라는 단어에 대한 의미를 개념 C를 표현하고 있다고 말함으로써 줄 수 없게 된다는 것이다. 개념 C와의 연관이 ‘C’의 의미에 본질적이지 않기 때문이다.

함축의 경우도 비슷한 사례가 적용되지 않는가? 함축이 어떤 단어, 혹은 발화에 detachable하고, cancellable하다는 것은 단어나 발화의 의미와 이 함축과의 연관이 본질적이지 않다는 것을 의미하는 것 같다. 하지만 넓은 의미에서라도, 함축이 한 단어의 의미의 일부라고 말할 수 있으려면, 함축과 단어의 의미 사이의 관계는 본질적이어야 한다. 만약 그렇지 않다면 위와 유사하게, 단어의 의미를 어떤 함축을 생산해낸다고 말함으로써 (부분적이라도) 줄 수 없게 될 것이다.

두 가지 직관이 분명하게 반대 방향으로 작용하는 것 같다. 함축은 우리 공동체의 관습적인 사실들에 의해 결정된다. 그러므로 단어의 의미에 함축은 우연적으로 덧붙여지는 것 같아 보인다. 함축이 detachable하고, cancellable하다는 사실은 이 직관을 지지하는 것 같다. 하지만, 함축에 무지하다면, 명백히 그 사람은 단어를 온전히 이해하고 있지 않은 것 같아 보인다. 즉, 함축은 분명히 단어의 의미의 일부를 이룬다. 하지만 단어의 의미가 함축과 가지고 있는 관계가 본질적이지 않다면 우리는 그 단어의 의미를 주는데 있어서 함축을 포함시킬 수 없다.
