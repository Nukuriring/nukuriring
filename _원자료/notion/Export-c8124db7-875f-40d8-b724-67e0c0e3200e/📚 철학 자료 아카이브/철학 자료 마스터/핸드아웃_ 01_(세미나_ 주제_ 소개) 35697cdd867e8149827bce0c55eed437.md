Three Questions

Question 1: What is self-knowledge?

Question 2: Is self-knowledge special compared with other kinds of knowledge? If so, in what respects?

Question 3: How should we understand the nature of self-knowledge so that we can explain (or explain away) its special features?

Question 1: What Is Self-Knowledge?

The Standard Notion of Self-Knowledge in Philosophy

Standardly, the notion of self-knowledge in contemporary philosophical discussions is understood as knowledge of one’s own mental states including sensations, thoughts, beliefs, desires, and intentions.

Knowledge of One’s Own Mental States(Self Knowledge), of Oneself, and of One’s Own Actions

Self-knowledge understood as knowledge of one’s own mental states should be distinguished from self-knowledge understood as knowledge of the self.

※ If Constitutivism and Constructivism about the self is correct, according to which the self is not a

persisting substance but what is contructed or constituted by one’s own mental states, then there

(구성주의에 따르면 자아는 지속하는 실체가 아니라 우리의 심리상태들의 총체가 자아다. 심리상태들에 의해 구성되어 있는 것이다)

Self-knowledge understood as knowledge of one’s own mental states should also be distinguished from knowledge of one’s own actions.

Question 2: Is Self-Knowledge Special? If So, in What Respects?

The Supposedly Special Features of Self-Knowledge

At least since Descartes, many philosophers have thought that we have ‘privileged access’ to our own mental states in the following two respects:

Epistemic Security: Self-knowledge is epistemicially more secure than our knowledge of the external world or knowledge of others’ minds.

인식적 안전성

A strong thesis for epistemic security of self-knowledge is that self-knowledge is infallible(오류불가능), indubitable(의심불가능), and incorrigible(수정불가능).

Few philosophers accept the strong thesis nowadays.

Still, many philosophers are still committed to the view that self-knowledge is epistemically more secure than our knowledge of the external world or knowledge of others’ minds. (상대적으로 더 안전)

Special method(얻는 방법): We acquire knowledge of our own mental states by a method that is fundamentally different from the methods by which we acquire knowledge of the external world or knowledge of other’s minds.

(perception, inference (deductive, inductive, inference to the best explanation, memory, etc)

The Problem of Self-Knowledge in Broader Perspective

From Descartes to logical positivism, it has been generally assumed that knowledge of our own mental states is the least problematic of all categories of knowledge, and that a central task of epistemology is to justify our knowledge of the external world and knowledge of other minds in terms of knowledge of our own mental states.

(1950 이전까지 자기지식은 가장 확실한 종류의 지식으로 여겨졌다, 그래서 덜 탐구되었다)

In today’s epistemology, however, it is no longer supposed that knowledge of the external world and knowledge of other minds are particularly problematic. Skepticism about these kinds of knowledge is no longer a major concern for epistemologists.

On the other hand, it has become difficult to explain the supposedly special nature of self- knowledge for various reasons, including

the rise of physicalism in metaphysics

Major Theories of Self-Knowledge

Acquaintance Theories (Russell, Chalmers, Gertler, Bonjour, Conee, Feldman, Levine, Pit)

Central Idea: We come to know our own mental states through acquaintance, namely through awareness of our own mental states that is both epistemically and metaphysically direct.

인식적으로 직접적(다른 것을 알아차림으로써 알아차리는 것이 아님. 현상과 실재의 갭이 없다.)

형이상학적으로 직접적(중간에 매개하는 것이 없고 직접적으로 알게됨, 인과적인 것이 아니다)

Acquaintance is epistemically direct in the sense that one is aware of one’s own mental states not by being aware of something else.

Acquaintance is metaphysically direct in the sense that no process mediates between one’s awareness of one’s own mental states and the mental states themselves. In particular, acquaintance is not a species of causal process.

Inner Sense Theories (Armstrong, Goldman, Lycan, Nichols & Stich)

Central Idea: We come to know our own mental states by a monitoring mechanism or a process

that scans our internal states.

우리는 우리 자신의 심리상태를 일종의 감시 메커니즘을 통해서 봄(지각과는 다름, 감각기관이 필요없음) 물리주의에 친화적

The scanning process of our internal states is similar to perception in that it is causal and contingent, although it is different from perception in that there is no specific organ dedicated to scanning internal states. (‘attention’)

Given that Inner Sense Theories understand introspection as a causal process, it is especially conducive to physicalism and naturalism in metaphysics.

Rationalist Theories (Shoemaker, Gallois)

Central Idea: The capacity for self-knowledge is an essential nature of rational thinkers, in such a way that anyone who is a rational thinker must be able to have knowledge of his/her own mental states whenever he/she is in these states. (Shoemaker)

우리가 우리 자신의 심리상태에 대해서 지식을 가질 수 있는 능력은 우연적 특성이 아니라 합리적 사고자로서의 우리가 반드시 가질 수 밖에 없는 것

In an influential argument, Shoemaker claims that Inner Sense Theories must be wrong because these theories imply that, since it is possible for a rational creature to lack the ‘scanning mechanism’ through which we acquire knowledge of our own mental states, there can be a rational creature that is nevertheless unable to have knowledge of his/her own mental states.

내적감각 이론: 스캐닝 메커니즘을 갖는 것은 우연적, 따라서 합리적 사고자면서 그러한 메커니즘이 없는 개체가 있는 것을 함축(자기맹 개체)

According to Shoemaker, however, such ‘self-blindness’ is impossible.

칸트의 초월적 통각(transcental apperception):

“There is a pen in front of me”  “I think there is a pen in front of me”

“It’s raining out there”  “I think it’s raining out there”

Rational Acency Theories (Burge, Moran, Boyle, Bilgrami, Coliva) (Agentialist Theories)

Central Idea: At least some of our mental states, in particular intentional states such as beliefs and intentions, express our agency in the sense that they are not merely things that happen to us but things we do. Therefore, recognizing these mental states as one’s own is not a purely epistemic matter. It also invovles seeing these states as committments for which one is responsible.

믿음, 욕구는 우리의 행위자성을 표현, 우리가 무엇을 믿고 있고 그런 것은 우리가 능동적인 정신 활동에 의해서 얻게 된 것

고통을 느낀 feeling은 수동적, 능동적인 정신 작용에 의해서 얻게 된 것이 아니라

따라서 내가 어떤 믿음, 욕구를 갖고 있는지를 인지하는 것은 순수하게 인식론적인 문제만은 아님. 믿음과 의도는 단지 발견이 아니라 내가 그것들에 대해 책임을 지니고 있는 개입

어떤 믿음 내 의도구나라고 인식하는 것은 인식론에서 그치는 것이 아니라 그러한 믿음, 의도에 대해 책임이 있다. (행위철학과 윤리학의 문제)

According to Rational Agency Theories, anyone who is a rational agent must be able to have knowledge of his/her own intentional states whenever he/she is in these states. For one can be responsible for what one believes and intends only if one can recognize them as one’s own beliefs and intentions.

믿음과 의도에 대해서 책임을 지려면 자기 믿음과 의도에 대한 지식이 필요

인간이 아닌 동물들도 믿음과 의도를 갖고 있는 것 같지만, 동물들은 자신이 갖고 있는 믿음과 욕구가 무엇인지에 대한 지식은 갖고 있지 않는 것 같다. 합리적 행위자성 이론가들에 따르면, 바로 이 때문에 동물들이 자신의  믿음과 의도에 따라 행동했을 때 동물들은 그 행동에 책임을 지지 않는다.

Expressivist Theories (Bar-On, Finkelstein)

Central Idea: The supposed reports of one’s own mental states are avowals rather than statements. That is, they directly express the mental states, just as wincing expresses one’s pain and smile expresses one’s pleasant feeling.

(1)   “I think it’s raining”

(2)  “I am feeling pain”

Simple Expressivism: ‘reports of one’s own mental states cannot be true or false!” (compare: Emotivism)

장점: 자기지식의 직접성을 acquaintance theory 처럼 신비스럽게 설명하지 않고 직접적으로 표출하는 것으로 설명

단점: 자기 지식이라는 말 자체가 잘못, 심리 상태에 대해서 알고 있는 것이 아니므로

Bar-On’s Neo-Expressivism: “We can talk legitimately that reports of one’s own mental states are true or false, although they are not really true or false” (compare: Blackburn’s Quasi-Realism)

If Expressivists Theories are right, then it follows that, strictly speaking, we do not have knowledge of our own mental states. In this respect Expressivist Theories are akin to Expressivist Theories in metaethics.

But it seems difficult to suppose that there are no facts about our own mental states, whereas it is much more plausible to suppose that there are no moral or ethical facts. If there are facts about our mental states, however, then we should be able to know them.

In sum, Expressivist Theorists seem to be in a dilemma: Either they must deny that there are facts about our own mental states, or they must accept that there such facts but maintain that, for some reason, we cannot have knowledge of these facts.

Transparency Theories (Dretske, Byrne, Fernández)

Background: In a famous paragraph, Gareth Evans apparently argues for the ‘transparency’ of mental states such as beliefs, in the sense that we get to know what we believe not by considering the belief itself but by considering the relevant facts in the world.

철수      <비가 내린다는 철수의 믿음>    <비가 내림>

비가 내린다는 자신의 믿음을 직접 인식의 대상으로서 보는 것이 아니라 비가 내린다는 세계의 사실을 파악함으로써 자기가 이 믿음을 갖고 있다는 것을 알게 됨

철수의 믿음이 투명한 것

안경알 비유: 안경알을 쓰고 있다는 것을 직접 아는 것이 아니라 다른 사물들이 더 선명하게 보이므로 안경알을 쓰고 있다는 것을 앎

눈을 뜨고 있다는 것을 눈을 뜨고 있다는 것을 봄으로써 아는 것이 아니듯이 비가 내린다는 믿음을 아는 것도 비가 오는 것을 믿음으로써 앎 (데카르트가 얘기했던 우리의 믿음이 투명하다(clear)는 것이 아님)

transparent procedure

“[I]n making a self-ascription of belief, one’s eyes are, so to speak, or occasionally literally, directed outward – upon the world. If someone asks me ‘Do you think there is going to be a third world war?’, I must attend, in answering him, to precisely the same outward phenomena as I would attend to if I were answering the question ‘Will there be a third world war?’. I get myself in a position to answer the question whether I believe that p by putting into operation whatever procedure I have for answering the question whether p.”

(Evans (1982), The Varieties of Reference, p.225).

In other words, it seems that beliefs are ‘transparent’ in the sense that one comes to know, for example, that one believes that it is raining outside not by recognizing this belief itself but by ‘looking through’ this (transparent) belief and recognizing that it is raining outside.

Central Idea (Byrne): We come to know our beliefs by reasoning according to the following ‘doxastic schema’:

p

Therefore, I believe that p

We acquire self-knowledge by this special method, which is very different from the methods by which we acquire knowledge of the external world. Also, reasoning in accord with the doxastic schema is self-verifying, for one’s acceptance of the premise, p, guarantess that one believes that p. Hence we can explain the two respects in which self-knowledge is special or we have ‘privileged access’ to our own mental states.

※ Moore’s Paradox: It sounds paradoxical to assert a setence of the form

“p, but I don’t believe that p.”

(e.g.) “It’s raining. But I don’t believe it’s raining.”
