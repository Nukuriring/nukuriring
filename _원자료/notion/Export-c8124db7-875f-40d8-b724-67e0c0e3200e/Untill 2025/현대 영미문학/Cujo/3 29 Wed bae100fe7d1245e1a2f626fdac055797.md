Horror and terror are two related but distinct concepts often used in literature, film, and other forms of media.

Terror refers to a feeling of intense fear, anxiety, or dread that arises from an impending threat or danger. It is typically characterized by a sense of uncertainty and unpredictability, as the source of the terror may not be immediately visible or understandable. For example, a person may experience terror while being chased by an unknown assailant, as they do not know who or what is pursuing them.

Horror, on the other hand, is a genre of fiction or media that seeks to elicit a strong emotional response from the audience, typically through the use of supernatural or gruesome elements. Horror often involves graphic violence, gore, or other disturbing imagery that is intended to shock and frighten the viewer. Horror can also evoke feelings of terror in the audience, but it is not limited to this emotion alone.

In summary, terror is a feeling or emotion, while horror is a genre of fiction or media that seeks to elicit intense emotions such as terror, fear, and revulsion
