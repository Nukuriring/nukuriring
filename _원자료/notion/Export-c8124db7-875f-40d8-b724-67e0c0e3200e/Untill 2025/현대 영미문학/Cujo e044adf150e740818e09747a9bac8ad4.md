# Cujo

소설은 캐슬락(castle rock)의 연쇄 살인마, Frank Todd에 대한 언급으로 시작한다. 그는 여러 건의 살인을 저지른 이후 체포되기 이전에 자살했다. 그렇게 그의 사건은 끝난 것처럼 보였으나, 그의 이름은 여전히 살아남아서 아이들을 겁주기 위한 이야기로 통용되었다. 소설은 “괴물은 죽지 않는다”(The monster never dies)”라는 경구로 이와 같이 이해할 수 없는 끔찍한 이야기가 생명을 잃지 않고 계속해서 유통되는 사건을 지시한다. 그리고 몇 년이 지난 1980년, 캐슬락에는 다시 한 번 죽지 않는 괴물이 등장한다.

네 살짜리 아이 Tad Trenton은 한밤중에 자신의 옷장에서 반인반수의 괴물을 보았다고 믿고 공포에 빠진다. 그의 비명을 듣고 잠에서 깨어난 그의 부모는 아이에게 그가 본 것이 그저 자신의 착각이었을 뿐임을 알려줌으로써 그를 달랜다. 그의 아버지는 담요 더미와 테디 베어가 어둠 속에서 어떻게 끔찍한 괴물의 모습으로 보였을 수 있는지 설명함으로써 Tad의 불안을 해소한다. 즉, 괴물은 그저 그의 마음이 만들어낸 것이고, 이야기에만 등장하는 것이라고 아버지는 말한다.(5)

The most interesting point of the novel’s description of how Cujo end up getting rabies is the fact that Cujo is described as having his own feelings, intentions, thought, self-consciousness and even capacity for inference and guessing. In other words, Cujo is described as a humanly figure(though not as intelligent as human being) which has body of an animal rather than an mindless, a-rational being.

This point can be shown by following quotes.

“Cujo **Knew** He was too old to chase rabbits.”(20, Emphasis mine) 

“Cujo worked toward the rabbit, out for sport rather than meat.” (21)

“Cujo pelted after it, already **suspecting** this was one race he wasn’t going to win” (22, emphasis mine)

“…Cujo didn’t hesitate”(22)

“Cujo felt several of the bats flutter against the third of him that had managed to get into the hole, and he became **frightened.”** (24, emphasis mine) (25)

“… trying to get rid of the dirt and watery green stink of limestonem **trying to get rid of that Baddog feeling**” (26)

These remarks confirm that story is describing Cujo as a sentient, intelligent, intentional, conscious, and (to some extent) rational being. Cujo is even capable of having the emotions such as guilit and shame. Here I am not trying to say that animals do not have an ability to render such “Humane” mental states. I am only trying to say this story presents Cujo as a being which is capable of what we “human” is capable of in our ordinary life. 

the significance of Such descriptions of Cujo can be appreciated in terms of providing a ‘interpretablity’ or ‘intelligiblity’ to actions done by Cujo. the story implies that this precise ‘intelligiblity’ will be siginificantly damaged or threatened by rabies. 

I believe that something can be turned into a ‘monster’ only when it loses its intelligibility for us. When we cannot describe someone, or something in terms of what he(it) is thinking or belieiving or intending or knowing but only be able to describe in terms of what it ‘seems to be thinking, knowing, intending, etc’, we are clearly admitting that it has lost its intelligibility for us. 

I think recognizing that descriptions of Cujo is constituting the intelligibility of the creature would be significant for understanding and figuring out what exact horror this story tries to present is.
