1. What do you think of the lawyer as the narrator? What characterizes his narration and how does it affect the characterization of Bartleby?

I find the lawyer, the narrator of the story, as an unreliable, dubious and vague figure.  

At first glance, *Scrivener Bartleby* takes the form of biography. However, the story tells us more about the narrator, the lawyer. He seems to be dedicated to writing a biography of Bartleby. Yet, he does not really believe that he could write a complete one because he thinks “no materials exist for a full and satisfactory biography of this man”(p.95). apparently, he admits that he cannot do full justice to Bartleby’s biography even before he starts writing one. 

The narrator describes himself as “a man who, from his youth upwards, has been filled with a profound conviction that the easiest way of life is the best”(p.95). Furthermore, he characterizes himself as “an eminently safe man”(p.95). He seems to define himself as a humble, ambition-less, normal man. However, in the same paragraph, he starts to talk about a affinity with John Jacob Aster. Surely, he points out that he does not “speak in vanity”(p.95). 

admittedly, there seems to be a tension. He talks as if he is a modest man, yet at the same time he claims that he has some connection with the significant figures. The lawyer does not make him explicit, but it seems quite clear that he wants to brag about his career with John Jacob Aster. This implies that we can not easily tell what he really endeavors to say by just examining the content of his speech. We need to focus on how he talks to find out his real desire and intention. In other words, we need to concentrate on rhetorics or narrative strategy in order to reveal what he wishes to say.

Thus, his narrating invites us to think about what has been revealed by his own statements. This leads us to think that his characterization of Bartleby would tell us more about the narrator himself rather than the Bartleby.
