I like how she proceeds with the defintion of the ‘corruption’ - she states: “To corrupt something is to do more than changing the major essence or nature of that thing but to strip it of its utility its functions and its pleasures” With this definition, she clarifies two things: (1) Here, She is implicitly stipulates her opponent’s claim and purpose. She reveals What is at the stake in this debate. - She settles out what should be argued of the social media if it is to be accused of corrupting Human Interactions. In her conceptual scheme, Propostion speaker needs to show that Social meadia is striping the utility, functions and pleasures of Human interactions. Whereas she only needs to show that it is not the case. (2) Moreover, this conceptual scheme of her puts more heavier burden of proof for the propostion speaker. If propostion speaker to claim that Social meaia does corrupt the Human interactions, he must have some kind of conception of the essence and nature of the human interactions, either explicitly or tacitly. For him to proceed, He needs to justify his conception of Human interaction, which, for it is all the more vague and vast concept, evades any kind of definite stipulation.

With this ‘stating the definition’ strategy, I think she mangages to hold the high ground in the combat.
