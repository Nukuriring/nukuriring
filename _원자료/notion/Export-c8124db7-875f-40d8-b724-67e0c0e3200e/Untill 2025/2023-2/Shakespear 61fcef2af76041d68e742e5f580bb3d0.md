**the 'objective facts' of Desdemona's adultery are not what unsettle Othello. This becomes clear as he utters to point out what does not upset him in this line: "I had been happy if the general camp, Pioneers and all, had tasted her sweet body, so I had nothing known."(3.3.347-8) : He is claiming that his own 'Knowing the fact', rather than 'the fact itself' is the cause of the hell he is suffering right now.**

**But What kind of hell, is he in right now? He continues to say : "Farewell the tranquil mind; fairwell content; / Farewell the plumed troops, and the big wars / that makes ambition virtue - O, farewell! / Farewell the neighing steed and the shrill trump ... Farewell! Othello's occupation's gone" (3.3.350-59). His line suggests that what he has lost by his ‘Knowing’ is his entire career.**  

**It seems puzzling to me. If Othello’s subjective ‘knowing’ is what brings himself down to his misery, then how come his ‘occupation’, - i.e his whole military career - becomes jeopardized? I think we could say: Military success and battle-field honor has rendered Othello’s life intelligible in Venetian society. Those terms literally defined Othello and on those terms Othello made sense of his life and condition. Hence, Othello is suggesting that not just his honor, but the intelligibility of anything at all about his own life will collapse by his own ‘knowing’ Desdemona’s infidelity. (”Perdition catch my soul / But I do love thee! and when I love thee not, / Chaos is come again” (3.3.90-2))**

 **It seems to me that What Othello wants to ‘know’ is not Desdemona’s obedience, but rather her true heart. To put in other words, Othello had no question of Desdemona’s fidelity, or had no wish to ‘know’ that Desdemona loves him truly before Iago’s counsel. (Because there was no need for knowing at all, thus there was no place for doubt. As doubt only reside in the place where knowledge is possible) Now, he has a wish to ‘know’ her love. I could say, what Iago planted in Othello’s mind is not a doubt itself, but the posibility or vision or idea of knowin one’s heart with “ocular proof”(3.3.362). He thinks knowing could save his own life from the “chaos” he created for himself but, the very urge to know Desdemona’s heart is what fixes him at impasse.**
