# Part I Debate Map — McDowell, Travis, Gersel, Ginsborg

*A one-page synthesis of the four Part I authors, keyed to three axes that recur across the walkthroughs.*

The Part I dispute is a single quarrel viewed from four angles. McDowell (1) and Travis (2) are the principals; Gersel (3) and Ginsborg (4) are the flanking reconstructions — Gersel pushing the normativity ladder *up* to rescue McDowell, Ginsborg pushing it *down* to undercut him.

## Axis A — the reasons ladder (where does experience's contribution sit?)

```
                 ABOVE rationality
        reason₄  ┃ psych. state making reason₃ relevant      ← GERSEL pushes here
        reason₃  ┃ a reason the subject appreciates AS SUCH  ←   (rescues McDowell)
   ─────────────╂──────────────────────────────────────────  threshold of
        reason₂  ┃ attitude making a belief 3rd-person        "responding to
                 ┃ intelligible (Davidson's dictum)            reasons as such"
        reason₁  ┃ consideration / truth-maker cited in favour
   ─────────────╂──────────────────────────────────────────
   primitive    ┃ grasp of the SHEER APPROPRIATENESS of a    ← GINSBORG pushes here
   normativity  ┃ response; sub-rational, pre-linguistic         (undercuts McDowell)
                 BELOW rationality
```

- **Ginsborg** is the *source* of reason₁/reason₂; she argues experience is only reason₁ → minimal empiricism fails → replace rational constraint with *primitive normativity* (below the line).
- **Gersel** adds reason₃/reason₄ and argues Minimal Empiricism needs reason₃ (above the line) → only conceptualism delivers it.
- Same lever, opposite directions.

## Axis B — stance on the rule-following / skepticism problem

| Author | Stance on the Kripke/Wittgenstein (and external-world) skeptical problem |
|--------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **McDowell** | **Denies** it is genuine. The "sideways-on" demand is confused; quietist dissolution by transcendental reminder (the Slogan). |
| **Travis** | **Dissolves** it. The how-possible question "disallows itself" (§3 "One Bad Question"); reductio in §5 "Thought's Demise." Bedrock = *instancing*. |
| **Ginsborg** | **Concedes and answers** it. Takes Kripke's threat as real; primitive normativity is the response (fn 40). The only author who grants the skeptic standing and then meets it. |
| **Gersel** | Uses the rule-following result *as a weapon*: no substantial gap-closing rules → the particularist cannot bridge → so generalities must be *given* (no gap to bridge). |

Cavellian frame (discussion file `03`): the *capacity* for skepticism = commutativity = the capacity for thought. So skeptical exposure is symmetric across McDowell and Travis; conceptualism buys no immunity. McDowell *refuses* the anti-skeptical sales pitch and pins it on Travis (ch. 1).

## Axis C — the "sideways-on" question (is there a legitimate how-X-is-possible question?)

McDowell's prohibition on the sideways-on view is the wall the others climb — from opposite sides:

- **Travis** climbs it to *deflate*: "how is instancing possible?" is One Bad Question; there is no gap, no Myth.
- **Ginsborg** climbs it to *build*: "how are empirical concepts acquired?" is a genuine transcendental question that McDowell evades; primitive normativity answers it.
- **McDowell** distinguishes a *bad* sideways-on question (instancing from nowhere) from a *good* fundamental question (how perception bears rationally on thought), and accuses Travis of conflating them ("Travis is oblivious to it," ch. 1).
- **Gersel** stays inside the conceptual sphere; he does not press a sideways-on question but reconstructs the constraint that makes one unnecessary.

## The single fault line

All three axes track one disagreement: **does generality have to be *given in experience* (the passive side), or can it live on the *thought / judger* side (the active side)?**

- **Given in experience (passive):** McDowell, defended by Gersel. → conceptualism; the gap is "already closed in experience"; self-scrutiny (first-form evaluation) is possible.
- **On the thought side (active):** Travis (the commutative/Cartesian judger "eo ipso needs none" of experience's conceptual help); Ginsborg relocates it again, to a *sub-rational* normativity in sorting/response.

Gersel's pincer (the decisive exchange): you cannot keep *pure particularism* **and** *appreciative self-scrutiny* on truth-conferring alone — the active capacity can only be evaluated against further active exercises (frictionless spinning). Travis's reply: the demand for first-form, passively-grounded self-scrutiny is a pseudo-task whose impossibility "displays the folly of requiring its completion." That residual standoff — *is the first form of evaluation a genuine requirement or a pseudo-demand?* — is where the whole of Part I funnels, and (per `03`) it is ultimately a *methodological* disagreement about where bedrock lies and what may be demanded of philosophy.
