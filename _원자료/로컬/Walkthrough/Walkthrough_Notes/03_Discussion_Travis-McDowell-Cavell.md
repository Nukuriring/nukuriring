# Discussion Threads — Travis, McDowell, and the Cavellian Coda

*Thematic notes developed alongside the Gersel and Ginsborg walkthroughs. These are not standalone walkthroughs of Essays 1–2 but the live argument we worked out — keyed to Travis (Essay 2, "The Move, the Divide, the Myth, and its Dogma," pp. 36–76) and McDowell (Essay 1).*

## 1. Truth-conferring vs the epistemic gap (does Gersel threaten Travis?)

A natural particularist reply to Gersel: "Travis confirms the *truth-conferring* of the particular, so there is no gap." Correct as a reading of Travis (Gersel's fn 25: Travis "accepts that truth-ensuring is sufficient for possession of reasons") — but it closes the wrong gap.

Two gaps. The **metaphysical** gap (does the particular make the judgment true?) Gersel *concedes is closed* (pp. 90, 94: "it may be metaphysically ensured that a particular does fall under a generality"). That is reason₁ / Very-Minimal Empiricism, never contested. Gersel's argument runs on the **epistemic** gap — between the particular *conferring* truth and the self-conscious subject *appreciating* that it does (reason₁ → reason₃). Truth-conferring gives reason₁ for free and reason₃ for nothing.

And on Gersel's own report, **Travis does not say "no gap" either** — he *concedes the gap* and takes the other exit (p. 98): he "acknowledges that this imposes a gap… cannot be breached in such a way that a subject can self-consciously appreciate this… Travis's way of tempering the worry… is by arguing that it cannot be a requirement on our theory of judgement that such a gap must be closed in a way we can appreciate. The impossibility of the task displays the folly of requiring its completion (2018, pp. 40 & 50)."

So Gersel's pincer closes the *middle*: you cannot have pure particularism **plus** appreciative self-scrutiny on the strength of truth-conferring alone. The threat is *conditional* — real against a particularist who wants to *keep* self-scrutiny, toothless against one (Travis) who *drops* the requirement (at the price of *blind* reliance on recognitional capacities). The live question is relocated upward: is McDowell even *entitled* to make reason₃-style self-scrutiny a condition on "having a reason"?

## 2. The commutative / Cartesian judger (가환적) — Travis's positive account

"가환적" = **commutative**, a Travis coinage, always paired with **Cartesian**. Together they are Travis's positive account of self-scrutiny — the very thing §1 identified as the real battleground.

- **Commutative** (p. 57): "In judging of that tree that it is a beech we may also, equally, be judging of being a beech, a way for an object to be, that the way that tree is is a case of an object being that way." A commutative judgment reads *both directions*: object → way and way → object. It is the grasp of the **instancing** relation. A sheepdog (Viriato) *tracks* sheep by fixed features but cannot think commutatively — he is blind to "conceivable ways for what looks like a sheep not to be one."
- **Cartesian** (pp. 57–58): Descartes' two marks of a judger — the ability to *stand back* and appreciate what bears on a way of settling a question (openness to novel defeaters), and the unbounded generativity of reason as a "universal instrument." "What thus marks us as Cartesian thinkers is also just what allows us, and not sheepdogs, commutative thought" (p. 59). Pia's openness is *unbounded* (pp. 60–61).

The sentence that collides with Gersel (p. 57): "For McDowell rational perceivers are provided by perceptual experience itself with information… **we are commutative judgers only thanks to such extra help; for me a commutative judger eo ipso needs none.**" McDowell: you can be a commutative/self-scrutinizing judger *only if* experience hands you generalities. Travis: commutativity is a feature of the **judger's conceptual capacities**, brought to bare particulars; it *needs none* of experience's conceptual investment.

This is a *stronger* anti-gap move than bare truth-conferring: it targets the *epistemic* gap (Pia *appreciates* instancing via unbounded openness, with no "substantial gap-closing rule"). It also tries to defuse Gersel's regress/infinite-list dilemma: the commutative capacity is *neither* a rule via further generalities *nor* a finite list, but an open-ended sensitivity. And it carries Travis's "too late" argument (p. 56): to *use* experience's supposed conceptual gift you must *already* recognize where the historical instances the conceptual — so the gift is either gratuitous (you could see for yourself) or unauthoritative. The real myth, for Travis, is "the autonomy of the conceptual."

**Why it does not simply win.** Gersel's pincer is built against exactly this (p. 98): the Travis/Brewer view grounds rationality in "the proper exercise of our recognitional capacities" — the *active* side. But Minimal Empiricism demands those active responses be *passively constrained*. If commutativity is an *active* capacity, evaluating one active judgment can only appeal to *further active exercises* — the *passive* given never serves as tribunal (the "frictionless spinning"). So Pia's unbounded openness handles the *second and third* forms of evaluation but, per Gersel, never the **first** (experience → judgment), because the generality lives entirely on the thought side. Standoff, located precisely:

- **Travis**: commutativity is the judger's (active, conceptual) capacity; demanding that the *passive given* carry generality is the pseudo-task whose impossibility "displays the folly of requiring its completion."
- **Gersel**: unless the passive given carries generality, the active judgment is never *passively* answerable, and Minimal Empiricism collapses.

## 3. Commutativity opens skepticism (the Cavellian turn)

Observation: the *unboundedness* that makes Pia a commutative judger **is** the structural source of skepticism. "For any given way of settling whether there are sheep before one, she is prepared to recognize their limitations" (p. 61) is, structurally, the recipe for a regress of doubt. Local defeater (shaved goat, scarlessly-removed pouch) and global defeater (dream, evil demon, envatted brain) are the *same capacity at different scope*. To be a commutative judger is *ipso facto* to be the creature for whom the skeptical question is formally available. The sheepdog is skepticism-proof precisely because it is thought-proof. **Exposure is the tax on thought.**

Cartesian irony: Travis lifts the "marks of a judger" from *Discourse* V — same author, same root, as methodic doubt. The cogito and the demon are one coin; you cannot borrow the Cartesian judger and leave the Cartesian shadow at the door.

Canonical home: **Cavell's "truth of skepticism"** (*The Claim of Reason*). Our relation to the world is not fundamentally *knowing* (securable) but *acknowledgment*; skepticism is the standing possibility of the *repudiation of criteria*, the *price* of meaning. A criteria-using creature is a permanently doubtable one. "A creature complicated enough to speak language and have thought" is one for whom skepticism is an inevitable possibility.

**The symmetry that hurts McDowell, not Travis.** It is tempting to read skeptical exposure as a cost of *particularism*. But McDowell's judger is *equally Cartesian* (the intelligent wolf, the obligatory step-back; Gersel's reason₃ *is* that step-back). You cannot have reason₃ without doubt₃. Within the volume, McDowell *refuses* the anti-skeptical sales pitch and pins it on Travis: his transcendental principles hold "as a condition for our thought to be genuinely thought — *that way round: not, as in Travis's account, that entitlement to the principles averts skepticism*" (McDowell, ch. 1). So the naive "McDowell = anti-skeptic, Travis = exposed" picture inverts. Both are Wittgensteinian quietists about skepticism's *standing*; they differ on where generality sits.

Asymmetry of where the doubt lands: for Travis, doubt is always at the *instancing* point and occasion-local (ordinary fallibilism, contextually answerable). For McDowell, because generality is delivered *in* experience, doubt threatens the deliverance of the conceptual capacity *as such* — which is why he goes *transcendental*. Conceptualism does not lower the skeptical stakes; it *raises* them.

## 4. The "how-possible" question and bedrock (the starkest disagreement)

Travis appeals to **bedrock** (Wittgenstein, *PI* §217: "my spade is turned… this is simply what I do"). Instancing is the relation he treats "throughout… as **fundamental**" (p. 62); the demand for a sub-instancing account "is already to disallow the question."

The real dispute is whether there are **one or two** how-possible questions.

- (1) Travis's "One Bad Question" (his §3 title): *how is instancing possible* — viewed sideways-on, "all at once," on an occasion presupposing no prior such feat (p. 62)?
- (2) McDowell's "fundamental question of perception": *how can perception make the world bear rationally on what to think?*

Travis **collapses (2) into (1)**: McDowell's Dogma commits him to needing a *legitimate* how-possible question whose answer is "Impossible," and there is no such question. McDowell **prises them apart**: he agrees the sideways-on instancing demand is bad (he is no foundationalist), but insists Travis has thrown out a good question with the bad. In his own words: about "how perception can make the world bear for us on the thing to think" there is "at least a case to answer. And so far from answering it, **Travis is oblivious to it**" (ch. 1).

So McDowell handles the residual question by "other measures" — a **transcendental/constitutive** substitution, the **Kant Slogan** (the unity of intuition is an act of the very function that unifies judgment), which "makes intelligible in a general way" that perception delivers objects to thought. He is not answering *how-possible*; he is replacing it with a *how-it-must-be-constituted* story. Travis's counter is a **reductio** (his §5, "Thought's Demise"): *if* the how-possible question were legitimate and its answer "Impossible," then "we are not thinkers. There is no such thing as that" — taking the question seriously abolishes the thinker who takes it, so it cannot be well-posed.

Both deploy *transcendental* reasoning, in opposite directions: Travis to **disqualify** the question, McDowell to **secure the conditions** under which the reformed question gets a constitutive answer. Same instrument, opposite employment — one dissolves, one answers.

Deepest point: **both reach bedrock; they disagree about what is *in* it.** McDowell's bedrock already contains generality (the Slogan; perception's form is conceptual). Travis's bedrock is bare *instancing* — the historical particular and a generality kept on the thought side. Not "is there bedrock?" but "does the spade strike the conceptual, or strike instancing?" And the mutual accusation: McDowell — Travis is *oblivious* (under-asks); Travis — McDowell *answers a misbegotten question* and thereby manufactures the very Myth (the "autonomy of the conceptual," p. 56). Each thinks the other failed to stop digging at the right place.

## 5. Cavell's "pseudo-argument," projection, and why "intelligibility past instancing" is confused

The burden Travis owes: why is intelligibility-past-instancing *confused* rather than merely *declined*? Cavell supplies the grounding.

**The dilemma** (Cavell, the skeptic's "best case" / the *non-claim context*): the skeptic stages doubt over a *generic* example, stripped of any practical occasion, because he wants a conclusion about *knowledge as such*. But "I know"/"I doubt" work only against a specific claim, basis, and point — the context he has evacuated. So: either he speaks within criteria (his doubt goes specific and loses global reach) or he keeps the reach (and "cannot mean what he wishes to mean" — a *magical air* of profundity that says nothing determinate). Make sense and the magic disperses; keep the magic and you stop making sense. His honest exits: rejoin the conversation, or fall silent.

**Transpose**: McDowell's "fundamental question," pressed to demand an *account of instancing*, is the skeptic's best case in epistemological dress. Concretize it → it becomes one of Pia's ordinary, answerable recognitional questions (no Myth). Keep it transcendental → it disperses into thin air. So "One Bad Question" becomes a *diagnosis*: the demand is a **failed projection** — question-shaped only because it borrows the sense of ordinary recognitional questions while suspending the contexts that gave them sense.

**The legitimizing vision** (Cavell's *Excursus on Wittgenstein's Vision of Language*): words are *projected* into endlessly new contexts, governed by no rule from above, only by shared "routes of interest and feeling… forms of life." Projection is *unbounded* — the same unboundedness that makes Pia a commutative judger — but it is *not self-licensing*: "we are open to new meaning, but we are not always convinced by novel ways of speaking." A projection can *fail to carry us*; uptake can be withheld. That asymmetry lets one call the global intelligibility-demand *confused* (a projection that wins no uptake) **without** a verificationist meaning-rule. It threads between the dogmatist ("you literally can't say that") and the skeptic ("openness licenses my doubt").

**The price** (the *truth of skepticism*): the dispersal is *not final*. The skeptic's impulse is the standing human wish for the unconditioned — knowing rather than acknowledging — so the magic *re-condenses*. The demand is "confused in form but truthful in motive." Borrowing Cavell therefore lifts Travis off his brisk Austinian footing onto Cavellian ground, where the answer to the demand is not dismissal but *acknowledgment*. And it does not rescue McDowell either: his Slogan tries to *cure* the groundlessness (close the gap into knowledge), which Cavell reads as *another form of the skeptic's wish*. Cavell gives Travis the *content* of the dissolution and grounds "confused"; shares with McDowell only the *seriousness about the grip*; and rebukes both terminal gestures — Travis's dismissal as too quick, McDowell's Slogan as too consoling.

## 6. Methodological coda — quietism, the "bloody hard way" (BHW), and the profession

*The Claim of Reason* ends not in a thesis but in a *question*: "But can philosophy become literature and still know itself?" The form enacts the point — a quietism that meant its own quiet would end in silence; Cavell ends in a question that reopens. Why literature (Othello): the *answer-with-clarity-and-ordinariness* model cannot finish, because skepticism about the *other* is not a knowledge-problem clarity can close but a standing possibility of *refusing acknowledgment*, which recurs — and tragedy is the form that does not pretend to finish.

Against quietism-as-quiet:
- **The wish for a final quiet is itself a form of the skeptic's wish** — a craving for the unconditioned displaced to the level of method. The philosopher who accepts perpetual fuss is *more* cured of skepticism; the one demanding final silence is still in its grip. Permanent wakefulness is the *success* of the anti-skeptical project, not its failure. (Local quiet is real — each particular knot gets untied; only the *global* final silence is a fantasy.)
- **"With reason": reason is the insomniac.** The faculty that sings the lullaby is the faculty that sounds the alarm — *commutativity*, the unbounded openness. A being finally quieted about skepticism would be Viriato, who never doubts because he cannot think. The fuss is the *signature of rationality*, not a symptom of unfinished work.
- **Criterion of "the right way":** philosophy done right ends in a question that reopens — not a thesis that closes (McDowell's cure) nor a dismissal that silences (Travis's "One Bad Question"). Both *want to stop*; their texts *close*. Cavell's does not — and the proof it is still *philosophy* (not mere literature) is that the waking is *with reason*.

On **the profession**: the dependency runs one way — being professional cannot *guarantee* the BHW, but the BHW can be *professed*. In the modern sense (produce closed, citable results) the institution selects *for* closers, and the BHW is hostable but not constitutable. In the older sense (*profiteri* — to avow, take a vow) the BHW is the only thing that could be a profession: a vow to keep the question awake (Cavell's career is the literal case). Strictly, the BHW is the *unprofessionalizable remainder* — you can accredit argument-forms, not *wakefulness*; the profession is the body, the BHW the pulse it can house but not manufacture. And the one who does philosophy *for love* (amateur, *amator*) alongside another trade may be freer for the hard way than the tenured closer, because nothing forces them to the terminus.
