# Review dossier for *Owning Up* (v3): Red-Team Panel + Referee Report

*Two adversarial roles trained on `ESSAY_Owning-Up_v3.md`. Part I is a panel of the theorists the essay critiques, each pressing the strongest objection faithful to their actual commitments. Part II is a journal referee evaluating the manuscript as a piece of scholarship — academic fit, citation quality, depth of mastery, and scholarly integrity. The two are complementary: Part I attacks the argument; Part II audits the workmanship.*

---

# PART I — THE RED TEAM

The essay's survival depends on four load-bearing claims: **(L1)** the knowledge-telos is a *substantive presupposition*, not a neutral statement of the explanandum; **(L2)** warrant is a *receptive* notion while first-person specialness is *non-receptive*, so one relation cannot be both (§4); **(L3)** the telos *generates* the field's problems and dropping it dissolves them (§5); **(L4)** owning "makes the self-ascription true when successful," yielding downstream knowledge without being a knowledge-relation (§7). Each panelist aims at one or more.

### Alex Byrne (empiricist transparency) — "you have mistaken effortlessness for non-receptivity"
The essay's "immediacy" and "authority" are supposed to be marks of a non-receptive relation. They are nothing of the kind. Reliable belief-forming processes are characteristically *unconscious, effortless, and gap-free* — that is *why* introspective self-ascription *feels* immediate and unmediated. So the very phenomenology the essay cites as evidence *against* a tracking relation is exactly what a good tracking relation predicts. Moreover the essay caricatures my view: the doxastic schema is the *subject's own* reasoning, not a transition that "brackets" her. And the §7 escape — "owning makes it true *when owning succeeds*" — is reliabilism with the serial numbers filed off: "when owning succeeds" is a *proper-function/reliability* condition. The essay has reinvented externalist self-knowledge and called it a stance.

### Richard Moran (agentialism) — "I am your counterexample, and you need the telos more than you admit"
Two charges. First, against L2: my deliberative entitlement is a *non-receptive* relation that nonetheless *warrants* — I answer "do I believe *p*?" by settling whether *p*, and my self-ascription is knowledge because I am *entitled*, as a rational agent, to take my attitudes to answer to my reasons. That is a non-receptive warrant. If your dichotomy says there can be no such thing, I refute the dichotomy; if you concede my case, your §4 engine stalls. Second, against L3–L4: you discard the knowledge-claim and keep only "answerability." But answerability is *answerability to how things are* — my avowal is accountable, criticizable, capable of being *wrong*. Strip out the truth-directedness and "owning" becomes mere endorsement, and the normative structure I built collapses into the expressivism I spent a book escaping. You have kept my word, "owning," and thrown away its content.

### Matthew Boyle (constitutivism / "two kinds") — "the practical/epistemic split is a distinction without a difference"
For a rational subject, believing *p* and being aware that one believes *p* are "two aspects of the same awareness" — a *cognitive* condition, not a bare practical stance. Your insistence that the distinctiveness is "practical, *not* epistemic" presupposes a separation that does not hold for the active attitudes: there is no owning of a belief that is not already a self-conscious *grasp* of it. So either your "owning" includes the cognitive aspect (then it is epistemic, and you are inside the telos), or it excludes it (then it cannot distinguish believing *p* from merely *resolving to defend* *p*, and your account under-individuates the attitudes it claims to give access to).

### Lucy Campbell (expression without expressivism) — "groundless does not entail non-epistemic; that inference is your whole argument and it is a fallacy"
Your §4 runs on the equation *groundless = non-receptive = non-knowledge*. But "groundless" means only *not based on evidence*. There is non-evidential, non-inferential **knowledge** — basic knowledge — and first-person privilege is a case of it, explicable by a distinctive *rational ability* to operate with the fact, which is epistemic without being evidential. You read my "open problem" as a confession that knowledge must be abandoned; it is the opposite — it is the announcement that the analysis is non-*evidential*, not non-*epistemic*. Your positive view is therefore either my view in disguise (rational-response, hence epistemic — telos intact) or a constitution claim you have not earned (Part II will press the metaphysics). Either way the dichotomy that powers your diagnosis is a non-sequitur.

### A Rationalist (Shoemaker / Smithies / Stoljar) — "your key premise, 'warrant is receptive,' is simply false"
This is the panel's sharpest blade. On the constitutive view, for a rational creature *being* in certain states *constitutes* knowing one is in them; the warrant is *constitution*, not sensitivity. That is a **non-receptive warrant** — a direct counterexample to L2's load-bearing premise. Worse for you: your own §7 thesis, "owning *makes the self-ascription true* when successful," is *itself* a constitutive (hence non-receptive) warrant. So by your own lights you have described a relation that is at once non-receptive *and* truth-conferring *and* the seat of specialness — precisely the relation L2 declares impossible. Your essay is a constitutivist account of self-knowledge that, for rhetorical reasons, refuses the name "knowledge." Drop the refusal and you are Shoemaker with worse hedging; keep it and you contradict §4.

### A Self-Interpretationist / Empiricist about error (Carruthers / Cassam / Schwitzgebel) — "your error-profile is empirically false"
You claim (mark i, §6) that self-knowledge fails as *self-deception*, not *misperception*, and treat this as evidence for owning over detection. The empirical record says otherwise: Nisbett & Wilson's confabulation results and Schwitzgebel's case for the gross unreliability of introspection show we *misidentify* our own attitudes and experiences through ordinary cognitive error, with no motivated disavowal in play. The error-profile is *mixed*, and a mixed profile does not favor your thesis. And your scope clause — "avowable, reasons-sensitive attitudes" — looks gerrymandered precisely to exclude the recalcitrant, akratic, and confabulated cases that are the empirical heartland of self-knowledge. You have drawn the boundary of the explanandum around the data that fit.

### Brie Gertler (defender of empiricism; acquaintance theorist; the field's cartographer) — "genetic fallacy, and a false dichotomy that omits my view"
Three meta-level strikes. (1) *Genetic fallacy*: that a shared assumption is *implicated* in each problem does not show it *causes* them, still less that dropping it *solves* them. The problems may track a real and irreducible tension between first- and third-personal access that no reframing dissolves; you have confused "present in every problem" with "the cause of every problem." (2) *The dichotomy is not exhaustive*: **acquaintance** is a relation that is neither inferential-receptive nor agential — direct, non-inferential awareness of an occurrent state, epistemic and of a state that is there anyway. You bracket sensations to hide this, but acquaintance plausibly covers occurrent judgments too, and then it is a non-agential, non-inferential *knowledge*-relation your trichotomy cannot place. (3) *Abandonment is unmotivated*: every account of every kind of knowledge faces objections; we do not therefore abandon "knowledge." Your "inference to the best diagnosis" is an inference to the diagnosis that happens to license a conclusion (a broadly expressivist one) you favored independently.

### A Deflationist about "owning" (a hard-nosed naturalist) — "name the fact, or withdraw the notion"
What *fact* obtains when I "own" a belief, beyond (a) having it and (b) being disposed to assert and defend it? If nothing, then "owning" reduces to *the belief plus a disposition*, and self-knowledge of it is knowledge of a state and a disposition — receptive, ordinary, telos intact. If something more, you owe its identity-conditions and its psychological reality. "Standing behind one's word" is a fine phrase and not yet a theory; until "answerability" has truth-conditions, §6 is metaphor doing the work of metaphysics.

### The coordinated attack (the one that should worry the author)
The panel converges on a single kill-shot aimed at **L2**. The premise *"warrant is a receptive notion"* is false: the rationalist's **constitution**, Campbell's **basic/non-evidential knowledge**, and Moran's **deliberative entitlement** are all, by their proponents' lights, *non-receptive warrants*. If even one of these stands, then a relation can be simultaneously non-receptive (hence able to seat first-person specialness) and warrant-conferring (hence able to yield knowledge) — and §4's claim that "the two roles pull apart" collapses, taking L3 with it (the telos no longer *must* generate the problems). And then the essay impales itself on its own §7: "owning makes the self-ascription true when successful" *is* a non-receptive truth-conferring relation — i.e., exactly the non-receptive warrant L2 says cannot exist. So the dilemma is exact: **either non-receptive warrants are possible — in which case §4 fails and the telos is acquitted — or they are impossible — in which case the essay's own positive thesis (L4) is incoherent.** The essay cannot have §4 and §7 both. That is the argument the author must defeat; everything else is survivable.

### Lethality ranking
- **Fatal if unanswered:** the coordinated L2 attack (constitution/basic-knowledge as non-receptive warrant) → §4-vs-§7 dilemma. *This is the paper's life-or-death front.*
- **Serious:** Gertler's genetic-fallacy charge against L3; Campbell's *groundless ≠ non-epistemic*; Moran's "you need truth-directedness for answerability."
- **Serious but containable:** the empirical error-profile objection (Schwitzgebel/Nisbett-Wilson) — the essay can retreat to "the *avowable* cases" but must then argue the restriction is principled, not protective.
- **Survivable with work:** Byrne's "immediacy = reliability"; Boyle's under-individuation; the deflationist's "name the fact" (answerable by specifying answerability's commitments).

# PART II — THE REFEREE REPORT

**Manuscript:** *Owning Up: Against the Knowledge-Telos in the Theory of Self-Knowledge* (v3).
**Recommendation:** **Revise and resubmit (major revisions).** There is a genuine and locatable idea here, executed with unusual dialectical control and honesty; but one load-bearing premise is undefended against an obvious counterexample, and the scholarly apparatus is not yet at submission grade. Both are fixable; neither is yet fixed.

### 1. 학문적 적합도 — Academic fit and contribution
The paper targets a real, well-defined debate and makes a contribution of a recognizable and valuable type: a *diagnostic* that re-describes a family of standing objections as symptoms of one shared presupposition. When such a diagnostic works, it is exactly the kind of paper a strong generalist venue (*Philosophical Studies*, *PPR*, *Mind*, *Phil Quarterly*) wants. Two reservations. (a) The contribution stands or falls with §4, and §4 currently rests on an undefended premise (Part I, L2); until that is shored up, a referee cannot certify that the paper *has* a result rather than a reframing. (b) The positive thesis is, by the author's own admission, close to Moran, Bar-On, and especially Campbell (2022); the paper's fit therefore depends on the *diagnosis* carrying the novelty, since the positive view alone would be judged derivative. Verdict: **fit is good conditional on §4 surviving and on the Campbell-distance being made decisive in the body, not the footnotes.**

### 2. 인용 수준 — Citation quality and adequacy
This is the manuscript's weakest dimension and the clearest sign it is a draft. Three problems. **(a) Secondary-source citation of primary claims.** A large share of the substantive attributions — Boghossian's trilemma, Boyle's objection, O'Brien's "too demanding," Coliva 2008, Valaris, Barnett, Stoljar, Reed, Doyle — are drawn, by the author's own note, "via Gertler 2024" (the SEP entry). Citing a survey for what the primary authors *argue* is acceptable in a seminar paper, not in a submission; it must be replaced by engagement with the primary texts, and several page numbers are missing or marked provisional. **(b) Under-engaged proximate work.** Given how close the thesis lies to them, the following are cited too thinly: **Rödl (2007)**, whose spontaneity/receptivity distinction is the *direct ancestor* of §4's central distinction and who is currently name-checked rather than confronted; **Boyle (2009)**, whose active/passive distinction prefigures the hybrid; **Campbell (2022)**, who needs a dedicated paragraph, not a footnote, since she is the nearest rival. **(c) Missing literatures.** §6 mark (i) makes an *empirical* claim (the error-profile is self-deception) while citing no empirical work; the contrary evidence (Schwitzgebel; Nisbett & Wilson) is not engaged. The epistemology-of-warrant literature on *non-inferential / basic / constitutive* justification — precisely what L2 needs — is absent. Verdict: **not yet at submission grade; a full primary-source pass is required.**

### 3. 공부의 수준 — Depth of mastery
Mixed, and the citation pattern diagnoses it. The *synthetic* understanding is high: regrouping eight accounts by the receptive/non-receptive character of their proposed relation, and seeing the objections as one tension, is a genuinely scholarly act that a merely competent reader could not perform. But the *textual* engagement is shallow in a tell-tale way: the paper masters the field *as surveyed* rather than *as written*. The signs are (i) the reliance on the SEP for primary positions; (ii) the absence of close reading of any single target in its own words (Moran, Byrne, and Campbell are characterized, not quoted and pressed); and (iii) the key premise of §4 ("warrant is receptive") asserted without engagement with the warrant literature that would immediately problematize it. A reader who had worked through Shoemaker and Rödl on constitution/spontaneity would not have left L2 undefended. Verdict: **strong at the level of architecture, under-evidenced at the level of texts; the gap is exactly where the argument is most vulnerable.**

### 4. 학자적 성실성 — Scholarly integrity
The manuscript's strongest dimension, and unusually so. The author (i) repeatedly distinguishes borrowed from novel ("I claim no priority for the bare idea…"), (ii) flags the constitution/infallibility pressure point in the very objection that exploits it (§7, "constitution-when-successful"), (iii) hedges the transposition and marks placeholder citations honestly, and (iv) confines the cross-domain companion to a single non-load-bearing footnote rather than smuggling it in. This is exemplary. Two integrity *duties* remain unmet, however, and they shade from diligence into honesty. **(a)** The empirical claim in §6(i) is asserted while the contrary empirical record goes unmentioned; selecting the error-profile that suits the thesis, without flagging the data that cut against it, is the kind of omission a referee reads as advocacy rather than inquiry. **(b)** Citing primary claims via the SEP, while honestly *marked*, still presents survey-mediated readings as if they were the author's engagement with the sources; integrity requires either doing the primary reading or foregrounding the limitation. Verdict: **conduct is candid and above the field average; complete the two outstanding duties and it is exemplary.**

### Required revisions (in priority order)
1. **Defend L2 or reformulate §4.** Confront the non-receptive-warrant counterexamples head-on (constitution: Shoemaker/Rödl; basic non-evidential knowledge: Campbell). Either (i) argue these are not genuine *warrants* in the relevant sense, or (ii) weaken the claim from "warrant is receptive" to something the argument can still use (e.g., "the *specific* warrant the telos demands — a warrant *of an independent state* — is receptive"), and show the weaker premise still generates the tension. Without this the paper has no result.
2. **Resolve the §4-vs-§7 tension.** State explicitly why "owning makes the self-ascription true when successful" is not itself the non-receptive warrant §4 forbids. (The most promising line: it is truth-*conferring* but not *warrant-conferring of an independent state* — owning makes it true precisely by *not* being answerable to a prior fact. Make this explicit; it is currently buried.)
3. **Replace via-SEP citations with primary engagement;** add dedicated treatment of Rödl, Boyle (2009), Campbell (2022).
4. **Engage the empirical literature** on introspective error (Schwitzgebel; Nisbett & Wilson) where §6(i) makes its empirical claim; argue the *avowable*-attitude restriction is principled, not protective.
5. **Answer the genetic-fallacy charge** (Part I, Gertler): give an independent reason — beyond "it is present in each problem" — to think the telos is *causally* responsible and that no rival diagnosis fits the same data.

### What would move it to Accept
A defensible §4 (revision 1), a clean resolution of the §4/§7 tension (revision 2), and a primary-source citation pass (revision 3). The integrity and the architecture are already there; the paper is closer than its citation apparatus makes it look, and further than its central premise currently licenses.
