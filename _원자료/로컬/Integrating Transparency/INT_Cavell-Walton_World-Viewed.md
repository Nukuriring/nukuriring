> **What this file is.** A *walkthrough companion + integration layer* that sits **on top of** the source digests in this project. This file supplies the two things that digest deliberately withholds: (1) a navigable **reading architecture** (the book's arc and recurring spine), and (2) the **Walton↔Cavell (and Bazin) integration** the digests reserved "for separate, later work." ## A. THE READING ARCHITECTURE (the arc, corrected to 19 chapters)

The book moves in **three waves** (the digest's own framing), with the 1979 essay re-stating the whole. Use this as the map; go to `KB_Cavell_The-World-Viewed.md` for per-chapter detail and verbatim quotes.

### Wave A — The Ontology (method + photograph/screen) · chs 1–4
- **Ch 1 An Autobiography of Companions** — method. Film's *nature* and its *importance* are one question; the data is one's own remembered experience; film was "the one live traditional art" that long *escaped* modernism. (Autobiography is not preface but evidence: a medium's ontology is recoverable only from its lived hold on us.)
- **Ch 2 Sights and Sounds** + **Ch 3 Photograph and Screen** — the ontological core in ~9 pages. Photograph is *of* reality; "**a painting is a world; a photograph is of the world**" [p.24]. Automatism "**overcame subjectivity … by removing the human agent from the task of reproduction**" [p.23]. "**Photography maintains the presentness of the world by accepting our absence from it**"; a world present to me to which I am not present "is a world past" [p.23]. "**The screen screens**" — hides me from the world, and the world's existence from me [p.24]. "Existence is not a predicate" [p.24].
- **Ch 4 Audience, Actor, and Star** — the human on screen. The audience is **mechanically absent**; the screen *performer* takes the role onto himself (vs. the stage *actor* working into it) → the individual/star, not the character, is ontologically prior.

### Wave B — The Myths (types, genres, their exhaustion) · chs 5–10
- **Ch 5 Types; Cycles as Genres** — "a cycle is a genre; and a genre is a medium" [p.36]; the type is fused to the photographed individual; genres are *mortal cycles*.
- **Ch 6 Ideas of Origin** — origin is not the apparatus but the *antecedent wish*; near Bazin's "myth of total cinema," film is "wished for" before invented.
- **Ch 7 Baudelaire and the Myths of Film** — *The Painter of Modern Life*: modernity = "the transitory, the fugitive, the contingent"; the flâneur who would "see the world … and yet remain hidden from the world" = the film viewer avant la lettre; Baudelaire's gallery (Woman, Military Man, Dandy) = film's myth-stock.
- **Ch 8 The Military Man and the Woman** + **Ch 9 The Dandy** — the gallery: three ways a viewable surface stands to a hidden interior (role-worn; surface-as-being; opacity-as-craft). The book begins to pivot from world-skepticism toward **other-minds**.
- **Ch 10 End of the Myths** — the myths lose *conviction*; the modernist break reaches film's content. Structurally guaranteed (the "eternal" type was mortgaged to mortal stars + finite cycles). "Within the last decade film has been moving into the modernist environment" [p.60].

### Wave C — Medium, Automatism, Modernism · chs 11–19
- **Ch 11 The Medium and Media of Film** — *medium* ≠ physical material. The medium = "**a succession of automatic world projections**" [≈p.72]; its *media* (genres/types) are **discovered, not given**. (Revises Greenberg: material is necessary, not the medium.)
- **Ch 12 The World as Mortal: Absolute Age and Youth** *(walkthrough omitted this)* — film depends on real, aging, dying people; the *absolute age* of a movie is the literal mortality it records (Swanson watching her young films); Hollywood, having lost conviction, can declare recurrence "only … by **nostalgia**." Pivots to the **myth of youth / modern romance** (the unsponsored couple; *The Graduate*'s bus ending).
- **Ch 13 The World as a Whole: Color** *(walkthrough's "Ch 12")* — when color becomes a **declared condition** of the medium it *neutralizes* the black-and-white "drama" through which personality/event were read, **de-psychologizing/un-theatricalizing** the world and projecting **futurity**. (Counterintuitive: more perceptual fidelity ≠ more *world*.)
- **Ch 14 Automatism** *(walkthrough's "Ch 13")* — the pivot term. Two fused senses: (a) **photographic** automatism (mechanical, hand-free reproduction) and (b) **modernist** automatism (an inherited convention a tradition lives by). Thesis: film was **given** its automatism rather than inventing one — hence its long pre-modernist life; modernism = the loss of given automatisms and the task of discovering new ones.
- **Ch 15 Excursus: Some Modernist Painting** *(walkthrough's "Ch 14")* — via Greenberg/Fried (flatness, the support, **acknowledgment**, **presentness**): modernism makes an art's *conditions* its *subject*; "acknowledgment is the home of knowledge." Film *constitutively* defeats Fried's **theatricality** (the screen excludes the beholder) — it *starts* with the presentness painting must struggle to win.
- **Ch 16 Exhibition and Self-Reference** *(walkthrough's "Ch 15")* — film *escapes* modernist "exhibition" because it is **screened, not exhibited** (each print an equal manifestation, no original/copy hierarchy); genuine modernism is not self-reference (exhibiting the self) but **acknowledgment**.
- **Ch 17 The Camera's Implication** *(walkthrough omitted)* — "**there is always a camera left out of the picture: the one working now**" [p.126]. "**The camera is outside its subject as I am outside my language**" [p.127]. Modernist film must acknowledge not its presence *in* the world but its **outsideness** to it — and, with color's loss of "the coherent world from which I am absent" [p.130], the skeptical reversal: from self-reference to "the harder acknowledgments of the camera's outsideness to its world and my absence from it" [p.133].
- **Ch 18 Assertions in Techniques** *(walkthrough omitted)* — a device becomes an **assertion/acknowledgment** only through the **integrity** of the whole work, never by being technically *integrated*: "**nothing is a 'possibility of a medium' unless its use gives it significance**" [p.133]; "**integration can itself be a device. There is no substitute for integrity**" [p.141]. (Renoir's camera refusing to advance in *Grand Illusion* = "the refusal to assert … where one man's life ends and another's begins" [p.145].)
- **Ch 19 The Acknowledgment of Silence** *(walkthrough's "Ch 16")* — the deepest condition to acknowledge is **silence**: "a silent movie has never been made" (silent film projected *a world of silence*, not a deficient sound film). Synchronization redefined as the criterion of *poetry*. The screen world is silent **to** us (non-address), the acoustic face of the Ch 2–3 asymmetry; "access is not address."

### Coda — "More of The World Viewed" (1979)
Re-states and defends the whole, centrally against Sesonske. Names the thesis: **film is "a moving image of skepticism"** — the senses "satisfied of reality while reality does not exist," because "viewing it is all it takes" [≈pp.188–189]; "viewing unseen … exactly the price of skepticism" [≈p.196]. Revises modernism (the modernist *penetrates the past* — an **arrière-garde**, not avant-garde). Closes on one frame of *Rules of the Game*: "the camera is the death of theater."

**The spine (threads that run the whole book):** automatism (two senses) · the wish to *view the world unseen* · presentness/absence **asymmetry** (present to me, I not present to it) · types → myths → their mortal end · conviction/fraudulence (Fried) · **acknowledgment vs. knowledge** · theatricality/presentness · silence. Every later chapter is one of these threads tightening.

## B. THE WALTON ↔ CAVELL (× BAZIN) INTEGRATION

This is the layer the source digests deliberately omit. Chronology: **Cavell 1971 → Walton 1984**. Walton **knows and cites Cavell** (see §B.3), so part of this is a *documented dispute*, not only my synthesis.

### B.1 The shared ground — automatism as the absence of the subject
All three thinkers locate the photograph's special purchase on reality in the **mechanical / belief-independent** character of its making — *the removal of the human subject from the causal chain*.

- **Cavell:** photography "**overcame subjectivity … by removing the human agent from the task of reproduction**" [p.23].
- **Walton:** to see through a photo is to have experiences "**caused … in a purely mechanical manner**" [p.261]; the image is **counterfactually dependent on the scene even holding the photographer's beliefs fixed** [pp.264–65] — "the artist draws his hallucination; the camera bypasses the photographer's hallucination" [p.264].
- **Bazin** (`TP_Cav`): the *objectif* / lens releases the image from human subjectivity; "man plays no part."

So the agreement is deep — at the level of the **mechanism**, not just the surface: *automatism = no believing/depicting mind between us and the world = genuine contact, not representation.* Both deny the photograph is a mere picture.

### B.2 The three-way fork — what the absence of the subject *secures*
Same mechanism, three different payloads:

```
                MECHANICAL AUTOMATISM  (image of the world formed without the human hand)
                            │   ← shared premise (Bazin, Cavell, Walton)
        ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
      BAZIN               CAVELL                WALTON
   the OBJECT's          the VIEWER's          genuine PERCEPTION
     being               condition               (transparency)
        │                   │                       │
 image participates    world present to me,     we LITERALLY see the
 in the object's       I absent from it;        object THROUGH the photo
 being ("is" the       the screen screens me;   ("see deceased ancestors");
 model); mummy-        film = a moving image    seeing ≠ knowing; agent-
 complex realism       of SKEPTICISM            neutral, epistemic
```
- **Bazin → ontology of the object** (realism; the image shares the being of its model).
- **Cavell → condition of the viewer** (skepticism; the world is given *in my absence*; the wish to "view the world unseen").
- **Walton → a mode of seeing** (transparency; we are in *perceptual contact*; deliberately drained of the egocentric drama).

### B.3 The documented contact — Walton fn.4 contests Cavell directly
This is the single best-grounded seam (an actual textual exchange, from `KB_Walton` fn.4):

- **Cavell's claim** [p.24]: "a photograph is *of* the world, a painting *is* a world"; you can ask what lies *beyond the frame* of a photograph (it has an answer in reality) but not (generally) of a painting.
- **Walton's three rebuttals:** (i) paintings have their own **fictional** worlds; (ii) paintings *of actual scenes* are also "of" the world; (iii) you **can** ask what's beyond a painting's frame — *in the fiction* ("smoke within a frame may indicate fictional fire outside it").

→ So even the famous Cavell slogan and Walton's "transparency" are **not the same thesis**, and Walton thought Cavell's contrast overdrawn. (High-value: a real disagreement to anchor a 〈보기〉 or a 비판 item, not a manufactured one.)

### B.4 The evaluative ladder — seeing → knowing → acknowledging
The cleanest axis for an integrated passage, and it is *built from both authors*:

1. **SEEING** — Walton's rung. Photographs are transparent: we are in genuine perceptual contact with the world (even the dead).
2. **SEEING ≠ KNOWING** — *already inside Walton*: "perceptual contact … has rather **less to do with acquiring knowledge** … than has sometimes been supposed" [p.269]; one can see through a *non-knowledge-producing* (flexible) mirror.
3. **KNOWING ≠ ACKNOWLEDGING** — Cavell's rung. The screen world is **present yet silent to me** (Ch 19); the other's interior is reached not by *knowing* a surface but by **acknowledgment** (Chs 15–19). "**Access is not address.**" [추정 — my formulation of Cavell's move]
4. **The destination** — for Cavell film's deepest stratum is acknowledgment (of the medium, the camera's outsideness, silence, the other). Walton's analysis, by design, never climbs past rung 2.

### B.5 Why they cannot simply be merged (the irreducible difference)
The *same* fact — no subject in the chain — is, for Walton, an **epistemic / agent-neutral / symmetric** fact (a thesis about perception, egocentric drama bracketed), and for Cavell an **existential / asymmetric** fact (the viewer's *exclusion* is constitutive: present to me, I not present to it; the *wish* is the point). Walton reads **no wish** in the mechanism; Cavell reads the **wish** as the reason the mechanism matters. This is not a contradiction to be resolved but the **fork itself** — and the fork is the passage.

## C. 지문 설계 훅 (수능 / LEET)

> 기존 `KB_Cavell` §8과 `KB_Walton` §13은 각각 *저자 내부*에 머문다. 이 절은 **교차-저자** 설계만 다룬다. 인용·페이지는 출판 전 원전 재확인 필수.

**(가)/(나) 통합 지문 골격 — 1순위 추천.**
- **(가) Walton 투명성**(지각적 접촉, 기계적 belief-independent 인과, 봄≠앎) / **(나) Cavell**(현존/부재 비대칭, screen 차폐, 회의주의·인정). 평가축 = **"사진의 자동성이 *무엇을* 확보하는가"** — 대상(Bazin)·봄(Walton)·관람자의 조건(Cavell). 3자 구도로 키우면 변별이 한 단계 더 열린다(§B.2 다이어그램이 그대로 골격).

**고변별 교차 함정(쉽게 붕괴하는 구분).**
- *봄(seeing) ≠ 앎(knowing) ≠ 인정(acknowledging)* — 사다리 3칸을 선지에서 뒤섞기(§B.4). Walton도 1→2를 끊는다는 점이 함정의 핵심(학생은 "Walton=소박실재론"으로 오독).
- *투명성(Walton "see through") ≠ "of the world"(Cavell)* — 비슷해 보이나 Walton이 fn.4에서 직접 반박(§B.3). "둘은 같은 주장"을 매력적 오답으로.
- *belief-independent 인과(Walton) ↔ "주관성의 극복"(Cavell)* — **같은 기제, 다른 의의**(인식론 vs 실존·회의주의). "기제가 같으니 결론도 같다"가 함정.
- *automatism의 두 의미* — Cavell 내부의 (a)사진적/(b)모더니즘적 자동성을, Walton의 기계적 자동성과 3중으로 혼동시키기(`KB_Cavell` §8의 2의어 함정을 교차로 확장).

**〈보기〉 적용 소재.**
- **죽은 이의 사진** — 두 저자가 같은 사례를 다르게 글로싱: Walton "우리는 죽은 조상을 *본다*"(p.251) vs Cavell "a world past"·부재 속의 현존. 한 진술이 *어느* 글로싱인지 판별시키기.
- **합성·역광·왜곡 사진** — Walton: 부분적 투명/필요한 정확성(mean_N)이지만 misleading 가능; Cavell: 그래도 '자동적 세계 투사'. 정확성 ⟂ 투명성 ⟂ 세계-현존의 3분.
- **컬러 vs 흑백(Ch 13)** — "지각적 충실도↑ ≠ 세계-현존↑"라는 Cavell 반직관을, Walton의 "transparency는 정확도와 무관"(p.273)과 교차. 둘 다 *닮음=리얼리즘*을 깬다 — 합의 지점으로도, 분기로도 쓸 수 있음.

**주의.** 이 파일은 *통합 레이어*다 — 출판 아이템은 반드시 `KB_Cavell`·`KB_Walton` 원전 인용으로 되돌려 검증. Cavell 산문은 비선형·맥락의존적이라(그 자신이 "incomprehensible" 인정), 한 수(手)만 분리하고 연결 논리는 출제자가 보충해야 한다(`KB_Cavell` §8 caution).
