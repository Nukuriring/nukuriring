## §0. The strategic situation — read before using anything below

**Where the two figures currently sit in the project.** Wittgenstein enters v5 only at one remove, through **Wright 1998, "Self-Knowledge: The Wittgensteinian Legacy"** — i.e. as the *deepest residual objection* (E6 / §5): authority is a by-product of the grammar of the avowal language-game, so explaining it "changes the subject" *away from knowledge*. The paper concedes this front is open. **Austin does not appear at all.** "Performative" occurs only inside the Coliva discussion (BRIDGE_workings). So both figures are under-exploited, and Austin is wholly untapped — that is the opportunity.

**The one thing to keep in view the whole time — the double edge.** v5 is deliberately *middle-ambition*: it keeps **"self-ascription is knowledge"** and rejects only **"receptive."** Almost every resource in Wittgenstein and Austin pulls *past* that line, toward **"not knowledge at all, but a move in a practice"** — which is precisely **Wright's conclusion**, the front you have chosen *not* to fight on. So these materials are high-value but two-faced. Each candidate below is tagged:

- **SAFE** — supports *non-receptive* without endorsing *non-knowledge*; usable as-is.
- **DOUBLE-EDGED** — establishes non-receptivity but, pressed one step further, delivers Wright's deflation. Usable only with an explicit firewall ("this shows the relation isn't a detection; whether it is still *knowledge* is the question I leave open").

The discipline is simple: recruit Wittgenstein and Austin to fortify **(a)** the moves you already make (idle-wheel, Authority-as-Standing, Moore-triangulation) and **(b)** the *second-personal* notion B1 leans on. Do **not** let them argue the conclusion you're holding in reserve for the sequel.

## PART A — AUSTIN (the larger opening, since he is absent)

### A1. "I know" as a performative — *giving one's word / one's authority* ✓
**Source.** Austin, "Other Minds" (1946), in *Philosophical Papers*. The thesis the secondary literature reconstructs uniformly: in saying "I know," the speaker does not *describe* a specially good cognitive state; she **gives others her word — her authority — for saying that S is P**, on a parallel with "I promise." The "prohibition" is the diagnostic: just as "I promise I will, but I may fail" is not false but *infelicitous*, so is "I know it is so, but I may be wrong." (Sabina Vaccarino Bremner, "'When You (Say You) Know, You Can't Be Wrong': A Defense of J.L. Austin on 'I Know' Claims," is the best recent defense and reconstruction.)

**Where it plugs in — §4 (B1), Authority-as-Standing.** This is the strongest single import, because it hands you a *canonical, pre-existing source* for the move you currently make by stipulation. v5 (B1) argues authority is "a normative standing, not an evidential reliability," and that the deference owed an avowal is **second-personal — owed *to her*, not merely to the truth** (P3-ii). Austin *is* that thought, in speech-act form: to avow is to *give one's word*, and the authority of one's word is owed to an addressee, not earned against a fact. The avowal's authority is the first-person reflex of the standing one assumes in saying "I know."

**Bonus — it unifies the two defended views.** Your §3 backbone says agentialism and expressivism are "two faces of one position." Austin's performative is *literally* both faces at once: a giving-of-one's-word is an **expression** (it issues from the state, not from evidence) *and* a **commitment** (it binds the speaker, who is now answerable). So "I know"-as-performative is a ready-made model of the single non-receptive relation your unification posits.

**Novelty.** The *materials* are standard (Austin 1946; Bremner; the IEP/SEP entries). What is yours to claim is the *deployment*: importing the evidential-vs.-giving-one's-word contrast as the source of the second-personal standing inside the **idle-wheel** architecture. I found no one running Austin's "I know" specifically as the engine of an idle-wheel argument against the receptive conception of *self*-knowledge — that wiring is open.

**Stress-test — DOUBLE-EDGED, and you must pre-empt the classic objection.** The sixty-year-old complaint against Austin (Bremner's target) is exactly your danger: *saying "I know" performs no act of knowing the way saying "I promise" performs promising* — so "I know" looks like a description after all, or else "knowing" drops out and we have mere word-giving (= Wright: practice, not knowledge). Two protections. (i) Use Bremner's defense — Austin **never** calls "I know" a *pure* performative; it retains a constative dimension, and Austin ultimately *repudiates the performative/constative dichotomy* — so "giving one's word" and "being right" are not exclusive. That is precisely the neo-expressivist shape you already want (truth-apt *and* expressive). (ii) Firewall: deploy Austin for "*authority is word-giving, not instrument-reliability*" (B1, SAFE use) and explicitly decline the further step to "*therefore not knowledge*" (Wright's, §5, left open).

### A2. The *descriptive fallacy* — a name and an ancestor for the receptive conception ◇
**Source.** Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962), Lecture I: the "descriptive fallacy" — the long-standing assumption that the business of an utterance is always to *describe* some state of affairs, truly or falsely.

**Where it plugs in — §3, the diagnosis.** Your central diagnostic object is the **receptive conception**: that "I believe p" counts as knowledge only by being controlled-by / answerable-to an antecedent inner fact it *reports*. That is the descriptive fallacy applied to *self*-ascription — treating an avowal as a measurement-report when it is (also) an act. Naming it this way buys two things: a recognizable lineage (you are extending a famous Austinian diagnosis inward, to the first person), and a clean statement of the idle-wheel intuition — the *descriptive/truth-conditional* dimension is real but **misses what the utterance does**, which is exactly your "reliability explains truth (Class A) but is idle for distinctiveness (Class B)."

**Novelty.** Framing/structural, not a new argument. Rhetorically strong; gives §3 a heading the reader already respects.

**Stress-test — mostly SAFE, one caveat.** Don't overclaim identity ("the receptive conception just *is* the descriptive fallacy"). A sophisticated empiricist can grant avowals have a performative dimension *and* still insist that, *qua knowledge*, they require a tracking relation. So Austin illuminates the diagnosis; he doesn't by himself refute the receptive theorist. Use as the frame for §3, with the actual work still done by the idle-wheel cases.

### A3. Moran's *own* Austinian turn — the assurance view, to cash out the second-personal ✓  *[DEEP CUT — intramural]*
**Source.** Richard Moran, *The Exchange of Words: Speech, Testimony, and Intersubjectivity* (2018). Moran's **assurance view**: telling is not transmitting evidence but *giving an assurance*; it generates distinctively **second-personal** reasons, binding speaker and audience as "complainant and complainee," and is developed explicitly out of **Austin** (and Grice).

**Where it plugs in — §4 (B1), closing the gap you currently leave.** B1's load-bearing claim is that the deference owed an avowal is *second-personal* — but v5 gives no developed account of what that second-personal standing *is*, and a referee will press exactly there ("'owed to her' is doing a lot of work — unpacked how?"). The elegant answer is **in-camp**: the author you are defending wrote the account. Moran's assurance theory of *testimony* is a worked-out theory of second-personal normativity in speech; the authority of a first-person avowal can be presented as the *reflexive* case of the assurance one gives in telling — I give *myself*, as it were, my word. You thereby (i) discharge B1's IOU, (ii) do it with Moran-on-Moran (rhetorically powerful, since the change-the-topic objection is *aimed at Moran*), and (iii) tie the second-personal directly back to Austin (A1), so A1 and A3 reinforce each other.

**Novelty.** Connecting Moran-on-testimony to Moran-on-self-knowledge is natural but, in this debate, under-exploited; running it as the *cash value* of B1's second-personal deference is a clean contribution.

**Stress-test — SAFE, with one flag.** Testimony is *other*-directed; you are transposing assurance to *self*-ascription. Flag the transposition rather than smuggle it — but note it is the same transposition Austin already licenses (his "I know" *is* word-giving in the first person). Risk is mild: you are borrowing structure (second-personal reasons), not a contested metaphysics.

## PART B — WITTGENSTEIN (anchor existing moves; manage the edge)

### B1. Wittgenstein's *own* Moore's-paradox treatment — anchor for the Moore-triangulation ✓
**Source.** *Philosophical Investigations*, Part II §x (1953). Key remarks: **"If there were a verb meaning 'to believe falsely', it would not have any significant first person, present indicative"**; "My own relation to my words is wholly different from other people's"; **"'I believe…' throws light on my state."** (Marie McGinn, "Wittgenstein and Moore's Paradox," is the developed secondary treatment.)

**Where it plugs in — §4 (B2), Moore-triangulation.** Your B2 is currently sourced to Byrne. Wittgenstein is the *origin*, and his framing is exactly the one you want: the present-first-person incoherence is a feature of the **use** of "I believe," not a detection that misfires. The "throws light on my state" remark is a gift — the first-person use *manifests* the state without *reporting* it, which is your "commitment, not measurement" in one line. Anchoring B2 in Wittgenstein (and engaging McGinn) deepens a section that currently rests on a single contemporary cite.

**Novelty.** Anchoring + secondary-literature depth, not new content.

**Stress-test — DOUBLE-EDGED.** Wittgenstein's reading is *grammatical*, which is Wright's home turf. Borrow the premise ("the asymmetry is non-receptive — a detection model predicts no special present-first-person incoherence") and stop. Do **not** inherit the further Wittgensteinian thesis that the asymmetry shows "I believe" is *not a knowledge-expressing assertion at all* — that is E6/§5, left open. State the firewall in a footnote so the referee sees you know where the cliff is.

### B2. The first/third-person **knowledge-ascription asymmetry** — a NEW idle-wheel case ✓  *[built from the passage you flagged]*
**Source.** The PI p.222 passage you singled out: "I can know what someone else is thinking, not what I am thinking. It is correct to say 'I know what you are thinking,' and wrong to say 'I know what I am thinking.'" Pair with **§246**: "it can't be said of me at all (except perhaps as a joke) that I *know* I am in pain… except perhaps that I *am* in pain."

**Where it plugs in — §4, as a THIRD idle-wheel case** beside B1-authority and B2-Moore. The structure is a direct cousin of your Moore-triangulation, and it is arguably *sharper*:

- A receptive/detection relation is **person-symmetric** — detecting my own mind and detecting yours are the *same kind* of act, differing only in ease of access. So a detection model predicts that **"I know what I am thinking"** should be the *paradigm easy* case (maximal privileged access), and "I know what you are thinking" the *hard* one.
- Grammar delivers the **reverse**: "I know what you are thinking" is in order; **"I know what I am thinking" is the out-of-place one.** Receptivity predicts the *wrong* person-asymmetry.
- Diagnosis: "I know what I am thinking" is idle/odd because there is **no epistemic gap to close, no instrument to consult** — which is just to say the relation is *not* a detection. Reliability is therefore idle for this asymmetry, exactly as it is idle for authority (B1) and Moore (B2). The new case *generalizes the idle-wheel test* — and the test's generalization is the paper's signature.

**Novelty.** This is the most genuinely *new* item in the memo: a fresh idle-wheel case, built from the very passage you're taken with, that widens the test's coverage of Class B. Worth building.

**Stress-test — DOUBLE-EDGED (sharply), plus a deflationary objection to answer.** (i) The Wittgensteinian gloss of "wrong to say 'I know what I am thinking'" is, in its native habitat, precisely *that it is not knowledge* (grammatically out of place). So this case, pushed hard, is the fastest route into Wright. Keep it at "receptivity predicts the wrong person-asymmetry → the relation isn't detection" and bracket "therefore not knowledge." (ii) The empiricist's deflation you must pre-empt: "'I know what I'm thinking' is odd merely because it states the *pragmatically obvious* — idle to assert, like 'I know I have hands' — not because of any deep non-receptivity." Your answer: obviousness predicts *redundancy*, not *impropriety across the person-axis*; "I know what you're thinking" can be equally obvious in context yet stays in order, so obviousness doesn't track the asymmetry — non-receptivity does. (This rebuttal needs one tight paragraph; without it the case is contestable.)

### B3. The **avowal (Äußerung) doctrine** — legitimating the expressivism you defend ✓
**Source.** *PI* §244 (the verbal expression of pain *replaces* the natural expression — the avowal as *Äußerung*, not report) and §246. The lineage to the view v5 defends is well documented: **Finkelstein, *Expression and the Inner* (2003)** and **Bar-On, *Speaking My Mind* (2004)** build neo-expressivism on this Wittgensteinian root; Finkelstein insists **Wittgenstein never denied avowals an *assertoric* dimension** alongside the expressive one.

**Where it plugs in — §2/§5, the expressivist flank.** Two uses. (i) *Pedigree*: the expressivist datum (immediacy, groundlessness) is not an exotic stipulation but the mainline Wittgensteinian account of first-person psychological utterance — worth one sentence of legitimation. (ii) *Defense against E4 (truth-maker worry)*: Finkelstein's point that the avowal carries an assertoric dimension *as well as* an expressive one is exactly what neo-expressivism needs to keep avowals truth-evaluable without becoming reports. The Wittgensteinian source shows expression and truth-aptness are *not* in tension — which is the hinge of the Bar-On position you protect.

**Novelty.** Legitimation/lineage, not new argument.

**Stress-test — SAFE as legitimation; DOUBLE-EDGED if over-pushed.** Stay with *neo*-expressivism (truth-apt + expressive). If you slide to *simple* expressivism (avowals not truth-apt), you hand E3 the collapse and contradict the paper's own commitments. Cite the Äußerung root; do not adopt the eliminativist reading of it.

### B4. *On Certainty* on "I know" — supporting texture only ◇
**Source.** *On Certainty* (e.g. the runs around §§504–550): "I know" has a use only where doubt and grounds are *possible*; where a grounds-challenge cannot get a grip, "I know" idles.

**Where it plugs in — §2, the groundlessness datum.** A supporting gloss on *why* the "How do you know?" challenge misfires for present-tense avowable states: not because the answer is privately certain, but because the grounds-game has no purchase. Pairs with Austin's felicity-condition treatment of "I know" (A1) — same phenomenon from the OLP side.

**Stress-test — use as texture, not a load-bearing move; deflationary-leaning.** Same firewall as B1/B2; OC is congenial to Wright. One or two sentences at most.
