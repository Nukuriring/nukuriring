# Formidable Objections to Agentialism & Expressivism — A Catalogue

*Stage-1 deliverable for the defense paper (blueprint: defend agentialism & expressivism by showing the standard objections assume the knowledge-telos). Compiled from two independent literature scouts + the project KB. Each objection is steelmanned. Primary sources and URLs are recorded; items reached only through the SEP survey or left unverified are flagged. The analytic value-add is §IV, the **telos-dependence audit**, which sorts the objections into those the paper's central move dissolves and those needing separate treatment — and so determines the paper's real structure.*

---

## I. The two views, charitably reconstructed

**Agentialism.** First-person access to one's reasons-sensitive attitudes (belief, intention) is grounded not in observation or inference but in *rational agency* — responsibility for, commitment to, and deliberative authorship of one's attitudes. Canonical: Moran, *Authority and Estrangement* (2001); also Boyle, Bilgrami, McGeer, Coliva (constitutivism). The transparency method (answer "do I believe p?" by considering p) is licensed, for Moran, by the deliberating agent's *entitlement* to take her attitudes to answer to her reasons.

**Expressivism.** An avowal ("I believe it's raining," "I'm in pain") is a groundless, evidence-free *expression* of the state it ascribes, not a report based on detecting it; its authority derives from its being an expression, not from its being a good measurement. Canonical: Bar-On, *Speaking My Mind* (2004, "neo-expressivism"); Finkelstein, *Expression and the Inner* (2003). **Note (correction from the scouts): Lucy Campbell is a *critic*, not a proponent** — her "expression without expressivism" (2022) keeps "expression" but rejects expressivism for an epistemic account. She is best treated as a formidable *objector* whose own positive view turns out to sit close to ours (§IV).

## II. Objections to Agentialism

**A1 — Change-the-topic / not-a-genuine-competitor (THE central charge).** *Gertler 2018, "Self-Knowledge and Rational Agency," PPR 96:91–109* (https://philpapers.org/rec/GERSAR; full text https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/brie-gertler/files/self-knowledge_and_rational_agency_8.pdf); SEP §3.7; Parrott 2015, *Phil Studies* 172:2215. **Steelman (Gertler's words):** "Knowledge ordinarily involves the knower… being appropriately *controlled by* what is known… our agency… concerns a direction of control that is the reverse… The agentialist emphasis on agency thus seems to *change the topic*, from how we *know* our attitudes to how we *shape* them." Responsibility is not a truth-maker; agency explains ownership, not warrant. **Structure:** P1 knowledge requires the knowing-state to be controlled *by* its object; P2 agency is control *over*; ∴ agentialism supplies no epistemology — "not a genuine competitor unless it explains how agency provides self-knowledge." **Damage:** the literature's *organizing* objection; SEP makes it the agentialist's "primary burden"; even friendly developers (Parrott) concede the framing by retreating to an avowedly non-epistemic agentialism. **The biting premise (P1) is the telos.**

**A2 — Transcendental argument proves capacity, not method.** *Gertler 2018 §2.3* vs Burge 1996 / Moran's transcendental wing. **Steelman:** "ought-implies-can" yields only that rational agents must be *able* to know their attitudes; it never yields that the *agentialist route* is the privileged one — empirical self-knowledge could discharge the same obligation. **Damage:** disables the transcendental *motivation* for agentialism; targeted at Burge/Moran, not constitutivists.

**A3 — "Too demanding" (O'Brien).** *O'Brien 2003, "Moran on Agency and Self-Knowledge," EJP 11:375–390* (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0378.00192). **Flag: reconstructed from SEP's gloss (O'Brien 2003:379–81) + Moran's 2003 reply; the primary article was not directly read.** **Steelman:** to get from the first-order question (whether p) to second-order knowledge (that I believe p), Moran needs me to treat my first-order reasoning *as evidence that I hold the belief* — a reflective grasp ordinary self-ascribers lack; so either Moran over-intellectualizes, or transparency under-delivers. **Damage:** serious but *answerable* — Boyle (2011): the transition is *not an inference*, so no demanding meta-cognition is required; the objection presupposes the inferentialist picture agentialists reject.

**A4 — Scope / reasons-insensitive attitudes.** *SEP §3.7 (sharpest); Borgoni 2015; self-interpretation tradition (Lawlor, Cassam).* **Steelman:** the deliberative mechanism reaches only reasons-sensitive attitudes; recalcitrant/akratic/wishful/entrenched attitudes survive deliberation, and deliberating returns the *rational* answer, not the attitude one actually has — so these are knowable only by self-*observation*, which agentialism brands second-rate. Agentialism is thus incomplete *or* must concede observational self-knowledge is genuine. **Damage:** the cleanest structural limit; evaded (not refuted) by McGeer's non-rationalist variant and by Boyle's "a recalcitrant fear has its own rational perspective" reply. **NOT cleanly telos-dependent — see §IV.**

**A5 — "Observation ≠ alienation"; detachment can enlighten (and Levy's inversion).** *Reed 2010 (PPR 80:164); Borgoni 2015; Doyle 2019 (PPQ 100:432); **Levy 2016, "'My Name is Joe and I'm an Alcoholic'," Mind & Language 31:265** (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mila.12105); McGeer 2008.* **Steelman:** Moran's load-bearing normative premise is observation→alienation. Reed/Borgoni deny the entailment (a belief I detect by self-observation is no less *mine*); Doyle: detachment can be *necessary* for self-understanding; **Levy inverts it empirically** — for addicts, rationalist "look to your reasons" self-knowledge is *counterproductive*, and *impersonal*, third-personal self-knowledge ("I'm an alcoholic") *restores* agency. So the capacity Moran calls alienating is, in the cases that matter, the route back to agency. **Damage:** the most *momentum-carrying* recent line; attacks Moran's distinctive premise (not just his epistemology) and arms it with empirical cases. Leaves the view looking *unmotivated*. **NOT telos-dependent — see §IV.**

**A6 — Deliberation *creates* rather than *reveals* (Shah & Velleman).** *Shah & Velleman 2005, "Doxastic Deliberation," Phil Review 114:497* (https://philpapers.org/rec/SHADD); constitutivist concession: Coliva 2012a. **Steelman:** deliberation *settles/forms* a doxastic state, terminating in a fresh judgment; it therefore cannot deliver knowledge of the *standing, dispositional* belief one already had when asked. Where dispositional belief and freshly-formed judgment coincide, transparency *looks* like revelation, but in the testing cases (recalcitrance, ambivalence) they come apart and transparency reports the *manufactured* judgment. Coliva, from inside, *agrees* — the self-ascription "creates" the commitment. **Structure:** P bites = self-knowledge must be of a standing antecedent state present at asking. **Damage:** subtle and deep; grants the phenomenon and reinterprets it as belief-*formation*. **Telos-dependent: the sting requires an antecedent independent state to be missed — see §IV.**

**A7 — Cassam's Disparity / Vulcanism.** *Cassam 2014, Self-Knowledge for Humans; rebuttal Boyle 2015, EJP 23:337* (https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/1/22557392/1/Boyle%20on%20Cassam-FINAL.pdf). **Steelman:** rationalist transparency works reliably only if one's attitudes are as they rationally *ought* to be (Vulcan); "the Disparity" (Kahneman, Nisbett-Wilson) shows we are not Vulcans; so rationalism is self-knowledge for a superhuman, and where it works it is covertly *inferential*. **Damage — with asterisk:** the most *cited* sweeping broadside, but **Boyle 2015 rebuts hard**: the world-directed question is just *whether p* (no mention of rationality), TM can be reliable even for irrational subjects (the bias warping the belief warps the ought-judgment too), and the "ought rationally to believe" premise was imported by a *critic*. "Rationalism about self-knowledge is a chimera." Decisiveness *contested*.

**A8 — Conflation of active and passive belief.** *Coliva 2008 (Ratio 21:13); Boyle 2015, 2019.* **Steelman:** agentialism models belief on action ("something we do for reasons"), but most belief is received/standing/sedimented, not deliberatively formed; the deliberative model fits the rare case of *settling an open question* and over-generalizes. Coliva: phenomenology can't tell a *judgment* from a *wish*, so the agential route can't individuate which attitude is self-ascribed. Boyle concedes the active/passive gap "in principle." **Damage:** taken seriously as internal pressure; turns on whether one accepts Boyle's "two aspects of the same awareness" denial of the gap. **Telos-adjacent (assumes a passive standing state distinct from the active judgment).**

**Agentialism shortlist (most often treated as decisive):** A1 (change-the-topic) ▸ A5 (observation≠alienation + Levy) ▸ A4 (scope) ▸ A7 (Disparity, contested).

## III. Objections to Expressivism

*Family stratification matters (Campbell 2022): Simple/Eliminativist (avowals not truth-apt) → Deflationist (true but adds nothing) → Parallelist/Neo (Bar-On: expresses both the state and genuine privileged self-knowledge, authoritative because expression). Different objections kill different members.*

**E1 — Cognitive-achievement ("groundless ⇒ not knowledge").** *SEP §3.8 (own voice); Brueckner 2006; Bar-On's reply, PPR 73(2)* (https://philosophy.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/122/2013/10/Bar-OnReplytoBruecknerOUP.pdf). **Steelman:** knowledge is a status that must be *earned* (evidence/reliability/justification); expressivism *defines* avowals by freedom from exactly this; so however reliably true, avowals are mere true voicings, not cognitive achievements (a trained parrot would qualify). **Structure:** P1 knowledge requires *some* epistemic achievement; P2 avowals are constitutively achievement-free; ∴ not knowledge. **Damage:** the price-of-admission worry the sophisticated literature exists to answer; Bar-On effectively concedes expression alone can't deliver knowledge. **P1 is the telos.**

**E2 — The expression–knowledge gap (Boyle).** *Boyle 2010, "Bar-On on Self-Knowledge and Expression," Acta Analytica 25:9* (https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037c-52db-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content). **Steelman (strongest single anti-Bar-On argument):** from the *avower's* standpoint — do I or don't I intentionally make a definite truth-evaluable assertion about myself? *Yes:* then (Generality Constraint) I grasp that I'm committed to a state of affairs, so how am I *entitled* to assert it with no evidence? "Expression" helps only if I *already know* I'm in the state — question-begging. *No:* collapse into classical expressivism's "alienated picture" (I learn my mind by overhearing myself). Boyle: "Bar-On has not yet explained how it can be reasonable for us to make avowals without evidence." **Damage:** the most damaging objection *specifically against Bar-On*; internal, dilemmatic, uses her own commitments. **Telos-dependent: the entitlement worry assumes the avowal answers to an independent state — see §IV.**

**E3 — Simple-expressivism collapse / deflationism's instability.** *Campbell 2022 §3.2 (https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/142892/); Finkelstein 2003 §6.4.* **Steelman:** "true but adds nothing" is unstable — collapses into Eliminativism (knowledge-talk is nonsense → no self-knowledge) or Parallelism. **Damage:** decisive against Simple/Eliminativist (which almost no one defends — Bar-On coined the label to *disown* it). Not our target view.

**E4 — Truth-maker worry (apt/sincere ≠ true).** *Brueckner 2006 + Bar-On reply; Bar-On & Wright 2024.* **Steelman:** avowals carry a presumption of *truth*, not just sincerity; expression underwrites only sincerity/aptness (a wince has no truth-value); Bar-On's "synthetic account" makes the avowed state *both* truth-maker *and* warrant — collapsing the gap between *being right* and *being entitled*, and leaving expressive success *contingent* (global self-knowledge failure not excluded). **Damage:** the hardest technical pressure on Parallelism; links to E1. **Telos-dependent: the "truth-maker ≠ warrant" demand is the receptive picture — on the owning view their identity is embraced — see §IV.**

**E5 — Campbell's Parallelist Dilemma (the cleanest recent refutation of Bar-On).** *Campbell 2022 §3.3.* **Steelman:** Parallelism wants genuine self-knowledge *and* a *non-epistemic* account of authority. Does the avowed self-knowledge rest on evidence? *Yes:* "How do you know?" reappears → asymmetry unexplained. *No (groundless):* then it is *already* epistemically asymmetric by itself, "an explanation of asymmetry comes for free, and there is no further work for a non-epistemic account" → expressivism *redundant*. So Parallelism is unmotivated; the correct view (groundless self-knowledge explained *epistemically-but-non-evidentially*) is "a version of the epistemic approach" — **not** expressivism. **Damage:** among the most decisive in current literature. **Crucial for us: Campbell's Horn B *is essentially our positive view* — she agrees groundless privileged self-knowledge is real; she relabels it "epistemic." The Bar-On/Campbell dispute is therefore substantially terminological — a defense resource, not a refutation of the substance — see §IV.**

**E6 — Authority is social-linguistic grammar, not knowledge (Wittgenstein/Wright: "changing the subject").** *Wright 1998, "Self-Knowledge: The Wittgensteinian Legacy" (https://crispinjwright.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/wright-1998-selfknowledge-the-wittgensteinian-legacy-2.pdf); SEP §1.4. (Citation fix: the sensation/privacy/intention Wright paper is 1991, not 1989.)* **Steelman:** first-person authority is *constitutive* of the language-game (a rule: "no, you don't" betrays a misunderstanding of norms), a *by-product of the rules*, not a cognitive accomplishment. So explaining authority via expression illuminates a feature of *practice* and *changes the subject* away from *knowledge*. Wright's wedge: avowals split into *phenomenal* (groundless, strongly authoritative — fit the wince model) and *attitudinal* (weakly authoritative, defeasible by self-deception — *don't* fit). **Damage:** agenda-setting; "the jaws of the trap the other objections spring." **Deep and NOT simply telos-dependent — it is a rival *deflationary* account of authority; needs the positive owning story — see §IV.**

**E7 — Scope (sensations yes, standing attitudes no).** *SEP §3.8; Wright 1998; Boyle 2009, "Two Kinds of Self-Knowledge," PPR 78:133.* **Steelman:** the wince-paradigm fits occurrent felt states; the avowals philosophers care about are *standing* attitudes one is not currently tokening ("I believe the climate crisis is real" at 3 a.m.) — nothing to "vent" — and exactly the states subject to self-deception, where authority is weak. **Damage:** serious coverage objection; contested (Bar-On extends via the belief case). **NOT telos-dependent; partly friendly to a scoped owning view — see §IV.**

**E8 — Campbell's own open problem (the "rational response" circularity).** *Campbell 2022 §5.5; SEP §3.8.* **Steelman:** to *explain* rather than presuppose privilege, "rational response" must be analyzed *non-epistemically*; Campbell provides no such analysis, so the account is circular (epistemic reading) or leaves its key term primitive. Campbell: "the most serious… threatens to undermine my entire approach." **Damage:** targets the *successor* (anti-expressivist) view, not expressivism. *Directly relevant to us: this is the gap our "owning" is a candidate to fill.*

**E9 — Empirical pressure on privilege (Schwitzgebel; Nisbett & Wilson).** *Schwitzgebel 2008, "The Unreliability of Naive Introspection," Phil Review 117:245 (https://www.faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/Naive1.pdf); Nisbett & Wilson 1977, Psych Review 84:231.* **Steelman:** introspective report is systematically unreliable/confabulated even for current phenomenology and the springs of action — a confabulation/interpretation model fits better than direct expression. **Damage:** real but most contested/deflectable (targets *causes/fine-grained phenomenology*, not simple present-tense state-ascriptions; fallibility ≠ refutation). A *pincer* with E1/E6, weak alone. **NOT telos-dependent.**

**Expressivism shortlist (most often treated as decisive):** E5 (Parallelist Dilemma) ▸ E2 (expression-knowledge gap) ▸ E1 (cognitive achievement) ▸ E6 (authority-not-knowledge).

## IV. The telos-dependence audit (the result that sets the paper's structure)

**Cross-cutting finding (both scouts, independently).** Almost every *central* objection to *both* views shares one load-bearing premise: that **knowledge requires the knowing-state to be epistemically grounded / controlled-by its object** — the receptive, "well-grounded true belief," achievement conception. For agentialism it appears as "control-*by*, not control-*over*" (A1); for expressivism as "groundless ⇒ no achievement ⇒ no knowledge" (E1). *That shared premise is the knowledge-telos.* The blueprint's central bet is confirmed: the canonical objections to the two non-receptive views rest on the same assumption, so a single move — showing the telos is optional — rehabilitates both at once.

**But honesty requires sorting the objections, because not all of them depend on the telos.** This sort *is* the paper's architecture:

**Group 1 — Dissolved by dropping the telos (the paper's clean core).**
- **A1 change-the-topic** — explicitly demands control-*by* (an independent state to be controlled by). Drop the telos (the attitude is not a fully independent state to be tracked) and the "not a genuine competitor" charge lapses: there is no receptive prize agency was failing to deliver.
- **A6 creates-not-reveals** and **A8 active/passive** — bite only if there is an *antecedent, standing, owning-independent* belief the deliberation must *find*. Deny that (the attitude is sustained, not found) and "creating the commitment" *is* the access.
- **E1 cognitive-achievement** and **E4 truth-maker** — bite only on the grounding picture (achievement required; truth-maker must differ from warrant). On the non-telos view, groundlessness is the mark of a non-receptive relation and truth-maker = warrant (the owning) is embraced, not a defect.
- **E2 expression-knowledge gap** (partly) — Boyle's "how are you entitled to assert without evidence?" presupposes the avowal answers to an independent state. If it doesn't (one owns rather than reports), the entitlement question is defused.

→ These are exactly the objections the literature treats as the **primary burden** (A1; E1). The paper's strongest claim is that its single move neutralizes the field's *central* objections to both views.

**Group 2 — NOT telos-dependent (need separate, positive treatment).**
- **A4 scope** and **E7 scope** — coverage objections. Reply needs the positive *owning* machinery (recalcitrant attitudes = owning-*failure*/breakdown cases; observation as remedial supplement), not telos-dissolution. (E7 is partly *friendly*: a scoped view that uses owning, not venting, for standing attitudes sidesteps it.)
- **A5 observation≠alienation + Levy** — attacks Moran's *normative* premise, and the empirical inversion (detachment restores agency) cuts against the anti-observation stance. The defense must *concede* observation is fine in breakdown cases (consistent with owning-as-default) rather than dissolve this.
- **E6 authority-not-knowledge (Wright)** — a rival *deflationary* account; the deepest. The defense partly *agrees* (authority isn't receptive knowledge) but must resist the "mere grammar" conclusion via the positive claim that owning is a genuine first-person relation grounding downstream knowledge.
- **E9 empirical** — needs the avowable-attitudes/owning-failure reply.
- **A7 Disparity** — already neutralized *internally* (Boyle 2015), independent of us.

**Group 3 — Allies in disguise.**
- **E5 Campbell's Parallelist Dilemma** — her Horn B *is* our positive view (groundless self-knowledge, explained non-evidentially). The Bar-On/Campbell dispute is largely terminological ("epistemic" vs not). We can recruit Campbell: the substance survives her dilemma; only the *label* "non-epistemic" is at stake.
- **E8 Campbell's open problem** — the non-epistemic analysis of "rational response" she lacks is what *owning* supplies.

**Upshot for the paper.** Lead with Group 1 (the canonical burdens, dissolved cleanly — this is the contribution). Then honestly take up Group 2 with the positive owning machinery (this is the harder, necessary work, and prevents the overclaim that *every* objection assumes the telos). Recruit Group 3. The earlier worry — that the paper might overstate "they all assume the telos" — is pre-empted: the claim is precise (the *central* objections do; here are the others and how they are met).
